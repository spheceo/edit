- WHAT A CUT-IN IS FOR (the team's Cut-In Guide): it makes the video easier to follow. Every choice comes back to one question: what do we have to work with here, and what makes it easiest for the viewer to follow what is being said? That means ORDER (the screen never shows something the video hasn't shown yet: a moment from minute 25 shown at minute 5 spoils it or confuses them; only the intro and a teaser may look ahead) and HELP (what's on screen supports the words: "if the narration says she called 911, the viewer should see something that helps them picture that"). The commonest failure is unrelated footage playing too long: "it reads as filler, and the viewer stops connecting the picture to the words". When a case isn't covered here, go back to the question.
- READ EACH CUT-IN FIRST (its words, the footage around it): is it a RECAP (what we just saw: show that scene again), a TEASER (points ahead: a little of the footage about to play) or BACKGROUND (the past, people we never see: there's usually no footage, so the Windows screen carries it)? Is the narration short and describing what we have footage of, or dense (a timeline, an account, many details, a quote)? What directly related footage is there? How long is it?
- TWO BUILDS, judged cut-in by cut-in, mixed across the video (about half the team's are the Windows screen, more often the longer ones). Both are one cover_cut_in each, all of a stage's in one apply_edit_plan; Edit does the timing mechanics (dissolves, the zoom, the card, the voice-over onto A1):
  - FOOTAGE ONLY when it's short and the words describe what we have footage of: 2–7 shots, a new one where the narration moves to something new (a full stop, a new idea: "a cut on a new idea tells the viewer something new is starting; a cut mid-phrase feels random"; time them to get_edit_context's word times), roughly every 2–4 s and never one held past ~4.5 s (Mom Leaves 5 Kids: "more clips", "change clips more frequently"), slowed (~0.5) on a heavy beat and nearer normal speed (0.75–1) where the words move. ONE zoom runs across the whole cut-in (cover_cut_in's push; never a push-in restarting on every shot: "zoomed out to zoomed in, zoomed out to zoomed in… have one big zoom").
  - THE WINDOWS SCREEN when it's longer or dense, or the footage is thin, blurred or unrelated, or for anything that isn't footage (THE WINDOWS SCREEN below).
  With no directly related footage, work down: footage of the exact thing; the same person or place from a moment already seen; something from the case on the Windows screen (a photo, a report, an article, the map); the narration's key points as notes. Switching between these across the video beats one approach for everything.
- WHAT TO SHOW: footage of what the words say, from the same scene or a very similar one, found with one find_in_footage (a phrase per idea, every cut-in at once; look_at_media only when a picture is in doubt). "Officers arrived" is them getting out of their cars, not a still of the building. NEVER AHEAD OF THE STORY: an arrest, cuffs, an injury, a find or a confession appears only after it has played in the footage (check each shot's source time against the transcript). EACH MOMENT ONCE: never the same stretch twice in the video (the audit's repeated_broll), and shots seconds apart in the source read as one shot stuttering. NEVER REDACTED B-ROLL: a stretch mostly pixelated or blurred is noise under narration (the audit's censored_broll; while censor_check_pending lists a file, review again before finishing); show the words on the Windows screen instead.
- THE WINDOWS SCREEN (the team's main motion graphic; a desktop shot in cover_cut_in, or op desktop elsewhere): a desktop on the team's wallpaper with three kinds of window, each by its own name: Video Player (footage), Photo Viewer (a picture), Notepad (notes). How it works:
  - Going in, the footage the viewer was watching carries on in a Video Player and the camera zooms out to reveal the desktop (the default: open true; it's the house transition into a screen most of the time, not always). The camera then glides to each window as it opens and drifts between: things move, it never just sits there.
  - Each window opens on the word that names it (at: that word's frame, or said: the words): "things appear as they're mentioned, landing slightly before the word". Several can open at once when the narration names them at once. One picture and one note: one Photo Viewer and one Notepad.
  - NOTEPAD notes are the narration's key points, very short ("3 potential suspects", "Jared, her former fiancé"), one bullet per point, typed on as they're said (Edit times each to the narration's words): "something the viewer takes in almost without noticing rather than reads like a page". Timelines, someone's account, key facts, a record, a quote's exact words.
  - PHOTO VIEWER for a picture that earns its place (below), labelled "actual image" or "illustrative image" (a look-alike). A picture is never shown any other way (the old cards, insets and profiles are retired).
  - VIDEO PLAYER for footage of what's being described, linking the document to the moment. To come out, give the window of the footage that's about to play and exit_into it: the camera ends zoomed into it and the cut to that footage is seamless.
  - LAY IT OUT YOURSELF so no two screens look alike: x, y (the window's centre on the desktop, 0–1), width (a share of the frame), z (which is on top); neighbours can overlap a little, as on a real desktop. Left out, Edit spreads them.
- PICTURES THAT EARN THEIR PLACE show something specific to this case the viewer needs to picture: people the footage never shows (a victim, an earlier partner; photos from any platform), something said about a person (twenty years on the force: her in uniform), a frame of our footage pointing at someone already seen, a previous mugshot when the narration covers a record (otherwise a mugshot is for the outro), evidence (a look-alike that looks like a real evidence photo, "never a catalogue shot of a kitchen knife", labelled illustrative), documents (reports, charges, court records, articles). Never a photo of a kind of place (a hospital, a police station): map_view the real place, since "a stock building could belong to any case"; never stock, AI-made or watermarked images, or someone who might only share the name.
- SENSITIVE CONTENT: gore and injuries blurred (black and white where it's blood); suspects never need blurring; children in footage are usually blurred by the department already: blur only young children (roughly under 14) it missed; a child victim's photo with the face blurred unless it's posted publicly in many places; children's full names can be used.
- EVIDENCE AND LISTS: when the narration names several things the footage never shows ("zip ties, rope, tape…"), collect_images once (a query per thing; photos that look taken on a phone, never product shots) and put them on an EVIDENCE BOARD (op evidence_board: framed photos on paper with handwritten notes, one continuous camera glide; each item's at = the frame of the first word naming it; illustrative: true where they aren't the case's own). It lays its own shutters. Without pictures of those things, don't flick through unrelated footage: hold one relevant shot or use the Notepad.
- THE DATE CUT-IN (the "On <date>…" that opens the story) opens on where it happened: map_view of the place the narration names (street or building; never a city from a mile up), seconds = how long it's on screen; while the date is said, op date_title {text "July 13th, 2024", start, end}; then the place lights up (map_view highlight_after, highlight_fade ~0.4 s) with op place_label {text: the street or place only, start, end, map, building_track}. Out of the map through a dip or a crossfade, never a hard cut.
- INTRODUCING A PERSON over footage (the narration says who they are, or picks one out of a group): keep the eye on them: a vignette that follows them (an adjustment {grade {saturation -0.6, vignette 0.75, vignette_size ~0.9, vignette_aspect ~0.65, focus_exposure 0.3, vignette_saturation -0.35}}, track_region target "vignette" on their torso) and a callout by AGE and ROLE ("15-year-old victim", never "This mother") in the house label (white Geist 600 ~44, black box, red border 2 px) set off to the side, with a leader from their neck (track_region target "leader" on THEIR face; no clear face, no leader), the words rising in and fading out. The first cut-in after a teaser that names the person just seen FREEZES on them (that footage at speed 0.05) before other footage. When the narration puts them on a date, the date comes first.
- THE QUOTE CALLOUT (2–4 a video, the lines that carry the story, when the narration points to them): their shot on one side, a black gradient on the other, their exact words (read_transcript, trimmed, curly quotes) beside them in their speaker colour (clip named "Quote · <their label>"), Geist 700 ~70, glow, rising in on the narration's word and fading out (the audit's callout_off_speech). Words that are broken or ungrammatical aren't quoted: put the point in a Notepad instead.
- CALLS AND SOUND WITHOUT PICTURE (911 calls, voicemails): a glowing waveform centred over the footage under a dark vignette (an adjustment {grade {exposure -1.9, saturation 0, vignette 0.9}}), both spanning the call; subtitles as everywhere.
- VOICE-OVERS: narration recordings go on with place_voiceovers (at the footage's loudness); once covered, cover_cut_in removes the card and moves the voice-over onto A1 in purple. A word the narration gets wrong: revoice_cut_in with the corrected text. A cut-in that drags (dead air, or the user says so): tighten_voiceover (dry_run first, only where it drags), or speed_voiceover 1.04–1.08 at most; both ripple the timeline, so do them before laying sounds.
