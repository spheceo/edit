- WHAT A CUT-IN IS FOR (the team's Cut-In Guide): it makes the video easier to follow. Every choice comes back to one question: what do we have to work with here, and what makes it easiest for the viewer to follow what is being said? That means ORDER (never show something the video hasn't shown yet: a moment from minute 25 at minute 5 spoils or confuses; only the intro and a teaser look ahead) and HELP (what's on screen supports the words: "if the narration says she called 911, the viewer should see something that helps them picture that"). The commonest failure is unrelated footage held too long: "it reads as filler".
- READ EACH CUT-IN FIRST: a RECAP (what we just saw: show that scene again), a TEASER (a little of the footage about to play) or BACKGROUND (the past, people we never see)? Short and describing what we have footage of, or dense (a timeline, an account, a quote)? What directly related footage is there? How long is it?
- FOOTAGE FIRST (bodycam and courtroom; an interrogation's bridges show what's said instead, the room only treated: its style's THE BRIDGES). Most cut-ins are footage: 2–7 shots of what the words say, a new one where the narration moves on to something new (a full stop, a new idea, from get_edit_context's word times), roughly every 2–4 s, slowed a little (the default 0.8; the team: "maybe we have been slowing it down a bit too much"; never slowed in an interrogation room, where nothing is happening either way), dissolving between moments under ONE zoom across the whole cut-in (cover_cut_in does it; never a zoom restarting on each shot: "zoomed out to zoomed in… for all of them"). Found with one find_in_footage (a phrase per idea, every cut-in at once). NEVER AHEAD OF THE STORY (an arrest, an injury, a find, a confession only after it has played), EACH MOMENT ONCE in the video (audit repeated_broll), NEVER REDACTED B-ROLL (audit censored_broll).
- A GRAPHIC ONLY WHEN FOOTAGE CAN'T SHOW IT: a person the footage never shows, the past, a document, a place, a timeline, someone's exact words. Pick the one that fits the moment, vary them across the video, and never the same picture twice in the video (audit repeated_picture). The team's graphics (copied from their references; all move slowly and smoothly):
  - FILM LOOK (op film_look): a picture or footage full frame under grain, scan lines and a heavy vignette, tagged "[ACTUAL PHOTO]" / "[REENACTMENT]" / "[ILLUSTRATIVE]": the simplest way to show one photo of a person or a place; put the picture under it as a full-frame clip with a slow push.
  - THE WINDOWS SCREEN (a desktop shot in cover_cut_in): several things at once, or a list of facts: Photo Viewers for pictures, a Notepad with very short notes typed as they're said ("3 potential suspects", "Jared, her former fiancé"), windows opening on the words that name them. The footage zooms out of a Video Player into it and the camera comes back into that same player on the footage that follows, so it needs no transition either side. About one cut-in in three at most (audit windows_overused); never for a cut-in footage can carry, and never with the same picture as an earlier one.
  - EVIDENCE BOARD (op evidence_board): the handwritten "paper" board for evidence and things the narration lists ("zip ties, rope, tape…"): photos that look taken on a phone (never product shots), a red note under each, one slow glide down.
  - ANNOTATE (op annotate): red handwritten notes with lines pointing at what's in a picture or a frame ("strangulation marks", "signs of trauma"), gore blurred beneath.
  - RED QUOTE (op red_quote): when the narration points to what someone said, their exact words in red beside them with a short line, the footage darkened round them. 2–4 a video, on the lines that carry the story.
  - CALL (op call): a 911 call or voicemail heard without picture: a glowing waveform over a darkened map or scene, the caller's words under it.
- A PICTURE THE VIEWER CAN READ: a person's photo comes as the narration names them and says who they are on screen (a File Viewer's name, a photo inset's name, a name typed on): a face nobody has introduced reads as noise. Every picture fills the frame (full frame, cropped not padded, or in a designed graphic over a picture that fills: audit letterboxed), and another outlet's mark on it is cropped past or blurred (audit watermarked_picture).
- PICTURES THAT EARN THEIR PLACE show something specific to this case: people the footage never shows, something said about a person (twenty years on the force: her in uniform), a previous mugshot when the narration covers a record (otherwise mugshots are for the outro), evidence (a look-alike labelled illustrative, "never a catalogue shot of a kitchen knife"), documents. Never a photo of a kind of place (show the real place on the map: "a stock building could belong to any case"), never stock, AI-made or watermarked images, or someone who might only share the name.
- SENSITIVE CONTENT: gore and injuries blurred (black and white where it's blood); suspects never blurred; children in footage are usually blurred already: blur only young children (under ~14) the department missed; a child victim's photo with the face blurred unless it's public in many places.
- THE DATE CUT-IN and places: map_view of the place the narration names (a street or building, never a city from a mile up): top-down (pitch 0) or angled; marker "dot" for a place and "building" for a building; tone "dark" when windows or labels go over it; op place_label {text: the place only} types its name in monospace on black; op file_viewer floats the people or footage of the place over it, each with their name ("LARRY SCOTT"). While the date is said, op date_title. Out of the map through a dip or a crossfade, never a hard cut.
- INTRODUCING A PERSON over footage: the house callout (their AGE and ROLE: "15-year-old victim") set off beside them with a leader from the neck (track_region target "leader" on THEIR face), and a vignette following them; rising in, fading out. The first cut-in after a teaser that names the person just seen freezes on them (that footage at speed 0.05) before other footage.
- LOOK AT WHAT YOU MADE as a viewer would, in the stage's one look: nothing covering something else (a window over a face, a note under a photo, two copies of the same footage side by side), nothing appearing abruptly, nothing the words don't support. "Any human doing this would see that this is stupid." Fix it before moving on.
- VOICE-OVERS: narration recordings go on with place_voiceovers; cover_cut_in removes the card and moves the voice-over onto A1. A word the narration gets wrong: revoice_cut_in. A cut-in that drags: tighten_voiceover (dry_run first) or speed_voiceover 1.04–1.08 at most, before laying sounds.
