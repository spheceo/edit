description: Covering cut-ins with B-roll: shots timed to the narration, the date cut-in's map, introducing a person, quote callouts, claim lists, phone calls, voice-overs.
---

- CUT-INS (the black narration cards) may carry stylised text and graphics.
- WHAT A CUT-IN IS FOR (the team's Cut-In Guide, Oct 19): it makes the video easier to follow. Every choice comes back to one question: what do we have to work with here, and what makes it easiest for the viewer to follow what is being said? Easy to follow is ORDER (the screen never shows something the video hasn't shown yet, outside the intro: B-ROLL NEVER GETS AHEAD OF THE STORY) and HELP (what's on screen supports the words: "if the narration says she called 911, the viewer should see something that helps them picture that"). The commonest failure is unrelated footage playing too long: "it reads as filler, and the viewer stops connecting the picture to the words". When a case isn't covered here, go back to that question.
- READ EACH CUT-IN BEFORE BUILDING it (its words and the footage around it), four things: 1) its kind: a RECAP talks about what we've just seen (show that scene or a very similar one: it anchors the words); a TEASER points ahead ("new information from a group of strangers": show a little of the footage about to play, those strangers, so the payoff feels promised; the only footage that may come before its moment); BACKGROUND is the past, people we never see, things long before the footage (there's usually no footage for it: the words and pictures carry it). 2) What the narration does: short and describing what the footage shows, footage carries it; dense (a timeline, what someone said, many details, a quote) is easier to follow with words on screen. 3) What directly related footage there is: if it exists, it's the first choice. 4) How long it is: a longer one needs more variety.
- TWO BUILDS, most videos mix both, judged cut-in by cut-in (about half of the team's cut-ins are the second, more often the longer ones: an observation, not a target): FOOTAGE ONLY when it's short, the words describe things we have footage of and 2–7 shots show most of it (it keeps momentum); WORDS AND PICTURES ON SCREEN when it's longer or dense: timelines, quotes, the past, people or places not on the footage, or footage that's thin, blurred or unrelated (it slows things down so the viewer can listen, and shows what we have no footage of). With no directly related footage, work down: footage of the exact thing; footage of the same person or place from a moment already seen; something from the case (a report, an article, 911 audio, an image, a map: evidence cards and boards, a DESKTOP PROFILE, map_view); the narration's key points as text (THE QUOTE CALLOUT, a CLAIM LIST). Switching between these across the video beats one approach for everything.
- A FOOTAGE-ONLY CUT-IN: shots change where the narration moves to something new (a new sentence, a full stop, a new idea: "a cut on a new idea tells the viewer something new is starting; a cut mid-phrase feels random"), slowed and normal-speed shots mixed (slowed for a heavy beat), dissolves between them, the B-ROLL LOOK so the viewer feels we've stepped out of the live footage, and out of it a dissolve back into the footage (a riser on the way out when the next footage is a strong moment). E.g. "He was taken into custody and placed in the back of the cruiser. Officers then turned to the witnesses.": the suspect in handcuffs, slowed; dissolve to him in the back of the cruiser; on "Officers then turned…", dissolve into the footage of officers with the witnesses, which plays on as normal.
- PICTURES THAT EARN THEIR PLACE show something specific to this case the viewer needs to picture: people the footage never shows (a victim, an earlier partner; photos from any platform), something said about a person (twenty years on the force: a photo of her in uniform), a frame of our footage to point at someone already seen, a previous mugshot when the narration covers a criminal record (MUGSHOTS ARE NOT B-ROLL otherwise), evidence (a look-alike is fine but it must look like a real evidence photo, "never a catalogue shot of a kitchen knife", marked illustrative), documents (reports, charges, court records, articles). Never a photo of a type of place (a hospital, a police station): show the real place on a map (map_view), since "a stock building could belong to any case"; never stock, AI-made or watermarked images, or someone who might only share the name; and the order rule holds for stills too.
- SENSITIVE CONTENT: gore and injuries blurred (a blur over them; black and white where it's blood); suspects never need blurring; children in the footage are usually blurred by the department already: blur only young children (roughly under 14) it missed; a child victim's photo can be shown with the face blurred, unless the photo is posted publicly in many places, then as it is; children's full names can be used.
- B-ROLL IN CUT-INS: use source footage for B-roll, not AI-generated images. A cut-in's voice-over is covered by B-roll that shows what it says, never generic footage. Read the narration, then find the moments that back it up in the source footage (find_in_footage with a phrase per idea, for every cut-in at once; look_at_media to see a moment only when its picture is in doubt): someone confused when the words say they had no idea, the wreck or a shocked witness when they say a devastating crash. Time each shot to the words it shows (get_edit_context's cut-in words give each word's timeline time): a shot showing \"the teenagers and the missing driver\" starts just before those words and lasts until they're said. A cut-in is ALIVE: a new shot every ~2–4 s, cutting on each thing the narration names (a person, a place, an action: "officers arrived" is them getting out of their cars and walking in, not a still of the building), never one shot held past ~4.5 s (the audit flags long_broll_shot). Mom Leaves 5 Kids' cut-ins held shots for 7–11 s and the team asked again and again for "more clips", "change clips more frequently", "show footage accordingly whenever we mention something": alive, not frantic. The shots are butted end to end and fill the cut-in from its first frame to its last, on a video track above the card. Each shot is picture only, slowed with native frame sampling (no pre-render): about 0.5 for a calm or heavy beat, 0.75 to 1 where the narration moves quickly (all 0.5 read as dragging: "this has to be sped up"), with a slow push-in (scale keyframes from 1 to about 1.08 across the shot, ease linear). Between shots of different moments the B-roll DISSOLVES (the team: "make a dissolve here"; cover_cut_in does it, 12 frames); cuts within one moment stay hard (PUSH-INS). The cut into the cut-in gets its transition from TRANSITIONS (never the vertical drop: the team isn't a fan of it). Do all of that with cover_cut_in operations in one apply_edit_plan for the whole stage (it places the shots, dissolves, push-ins and B-roll look, clears the card and moves the voice-over to A1): you choose the shots and their times; include each cut-in's particular extras (a person's vignette and label, a quote, a different transition and its sounds) in that same plan. Pick varied shots (different moments, angles, people, evidence): shots only seconds or a few minutes apart in the source, cut together, look repetitive, worst with a flash or shutter between them. Pick another shot, let one shot run longer, or, when the cuts really are one moment, treat them as one shot (PUSH-INS). Across the video too: when the footage has run out of fresh shots for a stretch and the same few moments would come back again and again, use something else there: a picture of the person (a found photo or a still_frame) with short text beside it (THE QUOTE CALLOUT's layout: their words, or a few of the narration's), found images of what's named (as evidence cards or a board: NO BARE STILLS), or a held shot under a CLAIM LIST.
- EACH B-ROLL MOMENT ONCE: never show the same stretch of footage in two cut-ins (or twice in one): viewers notice. Pick a different moment for each (find_in_footage gives several per phrase); the plan's audit flags repeated_broll.
- NO BARE STILLS: a picture (a found photo, a still_frame, a document) is never on screen as plain B-roll: not full frame with a push-in, and never letterboxed with black bars. Prefer footage B-roll; when a picture is the best thing to show, it goes on only inside a designed graphic: in cover_cut_in give it as a shot (an image's media; label: a few words under it, "The building on Capitol Avenue") and it goes on as an EVIDENCE CARD (the photo framed with a shadow over a dark blurred copy of itself, easing in and pushing in slowly, its label in the house label box); anywhere else the plan op evidence_card {media, start, end, label}; several named things, an EVIDENCE BOARD; a person's photo beside their words, THE QUOTE CALLOUT's layout; the outro's charges screens. No text callout over a picture or B-roll in a box of its own ("Another account: the window."): a person's words are a QUOTE CALLOUT, facts from an article go in the narration, and labels are the house label. The audit flags a picture on screen outside such a graphic as bare_still (letterboxed: true when it doesn't even fill the frame).
- A LABEL SAYS WHAT IS SHOWN, in plain words a viewer knows; never a source's name or abbreviation on it ("18 prior investigations · CT Mirror" left the team asking "CT Mirror means what?"): the outlet goes in the narration or nowhere, and where a source must be named (charges) it's spelled out as viewers would know it ("The Connecticut Mirror").
- MUGSHOTS ARE NOT B-ROLL: a mugshot is an arrest photo, for the outro's charges screens (and at most once to introduce someone the footage never shows, or a previous one where the narration covers their criminal record); never the picture under cut-in after cut-in (Mom & Teen's two mugshots stood in for B-roll in six cut-ins, before the arrest had happened). A person is introduced on footage of them (INTRODUCING A PERSON).
- A LONE STILL NEVER SITS ON BLACK: when a person is introduced (or their charges come up) and the only picture of them is a mugshot or a still, make it a DESKTOP PROFILE: apply_edit_plan op desktop_profile {start, duration, media (the photo), lines}: a blue desktop, the photo in a Photo Viewer window, the lines typed into a Notepad beside it, the windows scaling up one after the other, a slow push-in and a clean exit, all built in that one line. lines: their age and role first ("36-year-old mother"), then at most a few facts, charges only worded as a source states them (FACTS; never guessed); each line types on as the narration says it (Edit times them from the narration when line_at isn't given; give line_at only to place them yourself); to retime or reword a profile, run desktop_profile again over it (it replaces the one there), never rebuild its lines as text clips of your own (Corrupt Cop's lines, rebuilt one clip each, all came out centred); a line typed while the narration is elsewhere reads as out of sync ("Text needs to be more synced overall"; the audit flags profile_line_off_speech). Once per person; no label, leader or other text over it. It marks "desktop window opens" and "desktop line" for their sounds (a soft UI click on each window, music-and-sound). Mom & Teen's mother sat on the right half of a black frame with a label: the user sent it back.
- NEVER REDACTED B-ROLL: never choose a stretch of footage that's pixelated, mosaicked or blurred over most of the frame (a redacted interior, a blurred-out room) as B-roll: under narration it's just noise. A blur on a face or a plate within real footage is fine, and in the footage proper redactions stay (the sound of people speaking carries it). The audit flags censored_broll: replace the shot (find_in_footage has others) or show the thing with found images on an evidence board. Its check reads each file once in the background (started when the file's preview is made): while review_edit lists censor_check_pending, the check isn't done; review again before finishing, never ship on a pending check (Mom Leaves 5 Kids' blurred B-roll went out that way and came back three times: "this doesn't work when it's blurred"). WHEN THE FOOTAGE IS THIN OR BLURRED (a redacted interior, little to show for a long stretch), the words carry the cut-in: put what the narration says on screen (a CLAIM LIST of someone's account, THE QUOTE CALLOUT, an EVIDENCE BOARD with a title naming whose account or which record it is: "Nevaeh's account" with the window's picture, "Prior reports" for a person's record), so the viewer can follow along.

- B-ROLL NEVER GETS AHEAD OF THE STORY: never show something before the video has shown it happen. A person being arrested, cuffed, put in a car, hurt, or a find being made appears as B-roll only after that moment has played in the footage; before it, B-roll of that person comes from moments already seen or neutral ones (talking with officers, at the door, waiting). Check each shot's source time against where its moment plays on the timeline (the transcript you already read).
- B-ROLL SHOTS CHANGE ON THE NARRATION'S PHRASES: cut where the narration starts a new phrase or idea (e.g. on "As investigators…"), from its word times, not at arbitrary points mid-phrase; a new subject in the words = a new shot.
- PUSH-INS (the slow zoom across a B-roll shot and anything pinned to it; the moving map is linear by itself) are ease linear: a constant-speed zoom, a straight line on the graph, never in_out (no slow start, rush and slow stop). B-roll made of several cuts of the SAME moment (same scene and time, cut a little apart) is one shot: never restart the zoom on each cut; one continuous push-in runs across the whole run, each clip's scale keyframes starting where the previous clip's ended and climbing at the same rate (as if one parent above them were zooming: e.g. 1→1.04, then 1.04→1.08), and no flash, shutter, whoosh or drop between them.
- B-ROLL LOOK: B-roll reads apart from the footage: an adjustment over each B-roll stretch (the cut-in's span, beneath its text and callouts) {grade {saturation -0.6, vignette 0.3}}; a shot that gets a person's vignette (INTRODUCING A PERSON) carries that instead, desaturated the same way.
- EMPHASIS IN THE NARRATION gets several shots, not one: on words that stress how much or how many ("so severe", "a series of disturbing…", "again and again"), cut between 3–5 genuinely different shots in quick succession (~0.4–0.8 s each, timed to the words): different angles of the scene for severity (another camera, another person's view, a close detail; never frames seconds apart from the same camera, which read as one shot stuttering), different things (messages, places, people, evidence) for a series. The run shows what the words name: over "zip ties, rope, tape…" it only works if the project has footage or images of those things (look_at_media, found images). When the narration names objects, methods or places the footage never shows, VISUALISE THEM: collect_images for each named thing and pick photos that look like someone took them on a phone (the thing as it really lies: on a table, a floor, a workbench, in natural light), never studio product shots on white or black, a retailer's listing photo (a mop and bucket set, a boxed item), stock-photo gloss, watermarks or text (one collect_images with a query per named thing, not a call per query); show them on an EVIDENCE BOARD (apply_edit_plan op evidence_board: the photos framed on paper, a short red handwritten note under each: "zip ties", "rope", and the camera in ONE continuous glide down the board, easing a little as it passes each photo and picking up between, never holding or stopping (the team found hold-then-glide boards went "stop, go, stop, go"); one board per list: start where the board comes in, end where the next shot starts, and each item's at = the frame of the FIRST word that names it (word seconds × fps): the start of its phrase, "bags" in "bags of bloody bedding", never its last word, so each photo is centred as it's named and its note writes on as it arrives; the timing is the words', nothing to tune), with illustrative: true where they aren't the case's own; the board marks each photo ("board photo") and lays its own quiet shutter with the click on each at (never add shutters for its photos yourself; a transition's shutter on the cut into the board stands in for a photo within 8 frames of it). Mom & Teen's bedding board was heard wrong because its shutters followed two clocks: the first on the cut, just before "stripped", the second on "bedding", 0.8 s after "bags" began; never cut quick stills of a document or unrelated footage instead (the team rejected a run of search-inventory frames over a list of items), and over "a series of disturbing revelations" only with images that are those revelations (the scene inside the home, the evidence), never just other footage of the same people; if it doesn't, don't do the run (nor a shutter run anywhere it would only flick through unrelated footage): hold one relevant shot instead, or use THE QUOTE CALLOUT or a CLAIM LIST when the words are someone's (or, when the user wants research, find images of them). Their sound is part of the effect: a camera shutter on each cut (the run reads as evidence photos being taken; the team likes it), its sound only (a white flash on every cut strobes: at most one, on the first), each about 8 LU under the speech (a texture on the cut, not a hit: quieter than a lone shutter), with a short fade in and out (1 frame in, 2 out) so it doesn't click on and off, and lined up so its click lands on the cut; or one short riser across the run landing on a hit at its last cut. Never UI clicks, button presses or mouse clicks on cuts between footage: those are only for graphics popping up on screen (images in a file viewer, messages appearing), whatever the library's notes suggest for "a high pace".
- THE FIRST CUT-IN AFTER A TEASER names the person just seen ("This mother had left…"): FREEZE on them rather than cut to other footage: the same footage as a B-roll shot from the teaser's last moment (source_in where the teaser ended) at speed 0.05, which reads as the frame held, with the push-in, her vignette and her label (INTRODUCING A PERSON) on it; other footage only after the freeze has had its beat (the team: "just freeze the frame and use the same footage as we have just seen").
- INTRODUCING A PERSON over B-roll (the narration says who they are: "this mother"; or someone it names as a group, marking the one on screen, e.g. the boy among "the teenagers"): keep the eye on them. 1) A body vignette: an adjustment over their shot {grade {saturation -0.6 (as all B-roll), vignette 0.75 (the rest of the frame darker, not black), vignette_size ~0.9, vignette_aspect ~0.65 (tall, their whole body lit, not just the face), focus_exposure 0.3 (lifts them), vignette_saturation -0.35 (the surroundings greyer still)}}, its centre following them (track_region target "vignette", place "on", a box around their torso); make sure their whole body stays lit. 2) A callout: a label saying who they are by their AGE and ROLE in the case: "15-year-old victim", "17-year-old driver", never a vague "The teenager" or "This mother" (the age from the case facts: the script, transcripts or case_articles, see FACTS; if it isn't known, the role alone: "Victim"), in the house label style (white Geist 600 ~44 at 1080p, glow off, opaque black box pad_x 20 pad_y 12, red border 2 px [1,0.1,0.1,1], the same shadow as the place label), set off below and to the side with more room (right unless there's more space left), clear of their face and body and never over or beside anyone else (it would read as naming them, so the line never crosses another person), following their face (track_region on the label, place "below", offset ~[±260, 120]); a leader from the neck, just under the chin (style.leader {attach "side"}, default red line; track_region target "leader", place "below", the box around THEIR face: pick the person by who they are (clothes, the case), not by who's nearest; Edit follows that face with the face detector and says which faces it found; if it finds no clearly visible face in the box it refuses: give the box of the right face from its list, or, when the person's face isn't clearly visible, no leader at all (style.leader null): the callout is then a plain label beside them), a short diagonal reaching the start of the box: about 300 px at 1080p, the box roughly a hand's reach from their body, never far off across the frame. In a fast, shaky or zoomed-in shot, give the label's and the vignette's track_region calls the person as seen at the label's start, middle and end (`time`/`box` plus `boxes` at the other two; the leader follows the face detector): one sighting drifts onto the wall behind a face as the camera swings, three hold it to within a few pixels; where it answers that it lost them, add a box there. The line draws in, then the box fades in and the words rise in one by one (animation {preset "rise", duration 10, stagger 1.5}). It leaves as smoothly as it came, never just vanishing: fade_out 6–8 frames on the label (line and box fade together), ending with its shot or before. When the narration puts the person on a date ("On March 3rd, 2021, his daughter…"), the date comes first (as THE DATE CUT-IN shows one), then the person and their label after it, never the other way round.
- THE QUOTE CALLOUT: when a person says a line that carries the story (an explanation, an admission, a key claim: "I knew her…", "She did it") and the narration refers to it ("with an explanation already in hand"), show their own words big beside them. At most a few per video (2–4), on the lines that matter most; it can replace a weak emphasis run or repeated B-roll. 1) Picture: a shot of them from the footage (B-roll as usual: picture only, slowed with native frame sampling, push-in, the B-roll look), placed so they fill one side of the frame (x/scale). 2) Contrast: a solid {fill [0,0,0,1], gradient {from 0.62, to 0.12}} above it when they're on the right (black on the left, clear by 62%); mirrored {from 0.38, to 0.88} when they're on the left. 3) The words: on the dark side, close beside them at about their head's height, never over their face or body: their exact words from the transcript (read_transcript; never paraphrased; trimmed to the phrase that matters, "…" where it trails off) in curly quotes “…”, Geist 700, size ~70 at 1080p, color = their speaker colour (the colour of their subtitle label, SPEAKERS; name the clip "Quote · <their label>", e.g. "Quote · Tabitha", and Edit keeps it in their colour), glow {enabled, color null, radius 16, strength 0.55}, no box, no shadow, max_width ~0.45*width, one or two lines. Nothing else with it: no heading or context line over the quote ("Officer's observation", "About bedding and clothing"). It rises in (animation {preset "rise", duration 12, stagger 1.5}) on the word where the narration points to it (or as they say it), holds while it's talked about, and fades out (fade_out 8 frames), never just vanishing. Its start is the narration's word, never "after the graphic before it": when a graphic before it changes length or loses a photo, move the callout back to its word (Mom & Teen's "Like they mopped or something?" rose 2.7 s after "a floor that looked freshly mopped", over "out the back door", because the board before it kept the length of a third photo that was removed). The audit flags callout_off_speech: a callout whose words the narration says nearby, but not while it's up (said_at is where). The same next to an object for what was said on it: an officer holding a phone during a call, the caller's words beside the phone (the caller's speaker colour; white when they have none). House words are starred as in any text (HOUSE WORDS ON SCREEN).
- CLAIM LIST (sparingly, only where the narration lists a person's claims or their version of events: "she told officers she fed them every day, that the house was clean…"): each claim appears on screen as it's said, in THE QUOTE CALLOUT's layout (their shot on one side, the black gradient on the other): one short line per claim (a few words each, from the narration), left-aligned in their speaker colour (each line named "Quote · <their label>"), Geist 700 ~48 at 1080p, the same glow, each rising in on the word that starts it (voice-over word times) and staying until the list ends, then all fading out together. Once or twice a video at most.
- THE DATE CUT-IN (the "On <date>…" cut-in that opens the story) opens on where it happened: map_view of the place the narration names (the department, the station or the street; add the city and state), as a moving shot with the defaults and seconds = how long it's on screen, unless the user asks for another angle (each new view is a paid map session). Keep it close: a street or a building at the default range; where research gives only a town or a neighbourhood ("Van Nuys, Los Angeles"), look for the street the case happened on first, and failing that a range of 500–800 m, never past ~1,000 (a city seen from a mile up turns to a smear of tiny roofs that shimmers as the camera moves). Full frame at scale 1 from source_in 0, no scale keyframes: the camera slowly circles right and closes in. While the narration says the date, show it with ONE plan op, date_title {text: one line like "July 13th, 2024", start: where the narration starts the date, end: just after the spoken year}: it darkens the map heavily beneath it and sets the date large and centred in white with ONLY a drop shadow, rising in letter by letter and fading out. Never a box behind the date and never a glow on it (the team rejected a boxed, glowing date), and never build it by hand (the audit flags a boxed or glowing date as date_style). Then the darkening lifts and the red building marker and the place label come in together: set map_view highlight_after to that time in source seconds and highlight_fade to ~0.4 s, and add the label with ONE plan op, place_label {text: the street or place only (no city or state), start: the reveal, end, map: the map's clip id, building_track: as map_view returned it}: the house label (white Geist 600 in an opaque black box with a red border and soft shadow) just above the building, pinned to it as the camera moves. Never build it by hand. Then the cut-in's other B-roll, out of the map through a dip to black or a crossfade, never a hard cut (TRANSITIONS).
- A CALL THE NARRATION NAMES OVER A PLACE ("12-year-old Nevaeh called 911 from her family's apartment", over the map or the building): a small framed picture pops on over the building as the call is named: the caller (a found photo or a still_frame of them, never a minor whose face the footage redacts), else an illustrative phone dialling, as an evidence card's photo (smaller, over the place, not full frame), with a quiet phone-dialling sound under it (list_library; music-and-sound's levels for a small accent).
- CALLS WITHOUT PICTURE in the story (a 911 call, a voicemail): cover them with B-roll that moves slowly (a push-in), the glowing waveform and its dark vignette on top (see PHONE CALLS), and subtitles as everywhere after the intro.
- PHONE CALLS and other sound without a picture (911 calls, voicemails, recordings): a glowing waveform. Lay a dark vignette first: an adjustment layer {type "adjustment", name "Dark vignette", grade {exposure -1.9, contrast 0, saturation 0, temperature 0, vignette 0.9}} over the footage for the span (the footage still shows, darker at the edges), then a waveform on the track above it (both spanning the call, linked), centred, in its defaults (rich red tangled line, house glow). Scale its width, height and glow radius by the project height / 1080.
- VOICE-OVERS: audio files in the project that read the cut-ins (narration recordings) go on with place_voiceovers, never by hand. They play at the footage's loudness, so narration and what's said on screen sound equally loud: place_voiceovers sets that; for voice-overs already placed, run set_loudness on them (no loudness = match the footage). Each cut-in then plays its voice-over and is a B-roll spot (cover it with B-roll, or the card until there is some). A narration block's voiceover is its clip once placed. Once B-roll covers a cut-in from its first frame to its last (cover_cut_in does all of this), the card and its words were only placeholders: delete the black card and the words, and move its voice-over onto A1, the footage's own sound track (free there, since B-roll is picture only), coloured purple, which no other audio uses, so the narration sits in line with the rest of the sound and still stands out. Until then, leave the card and words in place.
- A WORD THE NARRATION GETS WRONG (a woman called "himself", a name misspoken): revoice_cut_in with the cut-in's corrected text (the whole cut-in); it speaks it again and fits the new take to the cut-in. A house word muted in the narration keeps its first sound ("bl-", as "bl**d" on screen), so it's heard as censored, never as a dropout (the team on a word cut out whole: "it just seems like there is a mistake in the editing").
- TIGHT VOICE-OVERS (only where a cut-in drags: the user says so, or its voice-over leaves dead air with nothing happening on screen; never as a pass over every cut-in): a pause over ~0.7 s inside a thought makes a cut-in drag; tighten_voiceover shortens such pauses to ~0.35 s and the B-roll follows. Not every pause: run it with dry_run on the stretch first, then only where it drags, never across a scene change or a deliberate beat (a pause before a reveal, after a hard line); give start/end to keep it to that stretch. Voice-overs are generated a touch quick already (the house voice at 1.15, as the team asked); one made slower (an older take, a recording) that the team calls slow gets speed_voiceover 1.05 across its cut-ins. A stretch that still feels slow after that (or one the user calls slow) can be sped up with speed_voiceover at 1.04–1.08 (its B-roll and graphics squeeze with it): slightly faster, never so it sounds rushed or rehearsed, never past ~1.1, and never over footage people speak in. Both ripple the rest of the timeline (remove_time is the same cut done by hand), so do them before laying sound effects and transitions on later cuts, or check those after.
