description: Building cut-ins: reading each one, footage first, the team's graphics and when each fits, the date cut-in's map, people, quotes, calls, voice-overs.
---

- WHAT A CUT-IN IS FOR: it makes the video easier to follow. Ask of every choice: what do we have, and what makes it easiest to follow what's being said? ORDER: never show something the video hasn't reached (only the intro and a teaser look ahead). HELP: what's on screen supports the words ("if the narration says she called 911, the viewer should see something that helps them picture that"). The commonest failure is unrelated footage held too long: "it reads as filler".
- READ EACH ONE FIRST: a recap (show that scene again), a teaser (a little of what's about to play) or background (the past, people we never see)? What footage is directly related, and how long is it?
- FOOTAGE FIRST (bodycam, courtroom; an interrogation's bridges show what's said instead: its style's notes): 2–7 shots of what the words say, a new one where the narration moves on (roughly every 2–4 s, never past ~4.5 s; audit long_broll_shot), slowed a little (0.8; never in an interrogation room), dissolving between moments under ONE zoom across the cut-in (cover_cut_in; never a zoom restarting each shot). One find_in_footage for every cut-in at once. Each moment once in the video (repeated_broll), never ahead of the story, never redacted footage (censored_broll). Thin footage: another moment of the same scene, a freeze, or a graphic, never a shot held long.
- A GRAPHIC ONLY WHEN FOOTAGE CAN'T SHOW IT (a person never shown, the past, a document, a place, a timeline, someone's exact words), the one that fits, varied across the video, all moving slowly:
  - FILM LOOK (op film_look): one photo full frame under grain and vignette, tagged "[ACTUAL PHOTO]", with a slow push.
  - THE WINDOWS SCREEN (a desktop shot in cover_cut_in): several things at once or a list of facts: Photo Viewers, a Notepad with very short notes. Where the narration lists findings (what a report says, a death, what was found), that's the place ("a notepad on a Windows screen… where we can write down that the baby was deceased"). Give no at or line_at: Edit opens each window and types each line a beat before the words that name them (times you set by hand land late). Make each one busy: 3–5 windows (never a lone Notepad: audit desktop_sparse) popping up one after another as they're named, at least a second apart, never two at once across the screen ("dangerous to have two things appear at the same time when they are across the screen"; who and what's said, a Notepad of the facts typed line by line: "better at utilizing these notepads", "have more images pop up"). The screen stays up until the footage it leads to starts ("stay on the scene… until the footage starts again"), over one wallpaper, no shot changing under it; a File Viewer holds at least 4 s over one shot (graphic_short; viewer_over_cut: one shot runs under the whole viewer, and may run long there) ("We keep the file viewer even while changing scenes"); a pop-up that adds nothing isn't one ("just stay on the notepad"). No "actual image" label in a window: that's only for a picture filling the screen ("remove actual image text on all these photo / video viewers"). At most one cut-in in three (windows_overused).
  - EVIDENCE BOARD (op evidence_board), titled with whose account or which record: things the narration lists, each note a few words that read without the narration ("cleared means what here?"): at most 4 photos, side by side and still, one slow push-in over the whole board and no sideways pans, each photo and its note coming in a beat before they're named ("far, far too rehearsed… a lot smoother", "slower so we can actually see what is going on", "more space in between the images").
  - ANNOTATE (op annotate): red notes pointing at what's in a picture.
  - RED QUOTE (op red_quote): someone's exact words in red beside them; 2–4 a video.
  - CALL (op call): a 911 call or voicemail as a glowing waveform over a darkened map or scene.
- A PICTURE THE VIEWER CAN READ: a person's photo comes as they're named and says who they are on screen (a File Viewer's name, a photo inset's name), and each person keeps that one photo through the video. Every picture fills the frame or sits in a designed graphic (letterboxed; a portrait keeps its face in frame), another outlet's mark cropped or blurred (watermarked_picture). Only what's specific to this case: never stock, AI-made or watermarked images, a kind of place ("a stock building could belong to any case"), or someone who might only share the name. Labels say what's shown, no outlet abbreviations ("CT Mirror means what?"). Someone named with no photo: a picture of them from the footage, else an illustrative stand-in (a relative's photo, the face fully blurred, labelled "illustrative image").
- ANOTHER OUTLET'S MARK (a channel's logo, a station's bug) on footage or a photo: op blur_marks, once, after the pictures are laid (it blurs every mark on the timeline with a blur stuck to its own shot: it follows the push-in, fades with the dissolve, ends with the shot); a mark that isn't another outlet's (the court's own room tag, a camera label) goes in its leave list once, and stays clear for good; never a blur rectangle of your own over a logo, which stays on after its shot ("Blur stays on the screen for no reason… everytime there's a transition in footage it happens… make it sticky", Marius; audit blur_off_shot).
- SENSITIVE: gore and injuries blurred; suspects never blurred; a young child the department missed blurred; a child victim's photo blurred unless it's public in many places. A blur covers only what it hides, tight on the face and following it (track_region), the whole face every frame, and none where nothing needs hiding ("only cover the child's face. Otherwise, it interferes too much").
- THE DATE CUT-IN: map_view of the exact place (a street or building, never a city from a mile up), marker "dot" or "building", tone "dark" under windows or labels; op place_label types its name; op file_viewer floats the people over it with their names; op date_title as the date is said. Out of the map through a dip or a crossfade. Look at its first frame once: the marker must sit on the place (else map_view again with the building's lat/lon). A moving shot's `seconds` is what its clips use plus one (≤12). Don't wait on a map.
- INTRODUCING A PERSON over footage: the house callout (age and role: "15-year-old victim") beside them with a leader from the neck (track_region target "leader" on their face). It lasts only while they're in that shot: Edit ends a callout or spotlight at the first cut under it (Marc: "Text is still visible here despite not pointing to Timothy"); for longer, put another on them in the next shot. A blur does the same: it ends with its shot or is keyed onto its target in the next (audit blur_off_shot). The first cut-in after a teaser that names the person just seen freezes on them (speed 0.05) first.
- LOOK AT WHAT YOU MADE once, as a viewer: nothing covering something else, nothing abrupt, nothing the words don't support. "Any human doing this would see that this is stupid."
- VOICE-OVERS: place_voiceovers; cover_cut_in moves the voice-over to A1. A word wrong: revoice_cut_in. A cut-in that drags: tighten_voiceover (dry_run first) or speed_voiceover 1.04–1.08, before laying sounds.
