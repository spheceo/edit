THE EDITING WORKFLOW: the whole edit, start to finish, in this one turn. It starts when the user asks for it ("start the edit", "start the editing workflow", "edit this video"); whatever else their message says (a case's tone, what to stress, what to leave out, a source to use) steers every stage. Work through every stage without stopping to ask: decide yourself, and keep a short list of what you weren't sure about for the summary at the end. Watching the footage (who says each line) started in the background when you loaded this skill, so it's usually cached by the subtitles stage.

The whole edit should take about ten minutes. That's a handful of tool calls per stage, not dozens: each tool call is a model round trip, the slowest thing there is. Per stage: load its skill (once; load it again only if its text has dropped out of view), get_edit_context narrowed to the stage, decide everything the stage needs, apply the timeline changes in one apply_edit_plan and the tools that place sounds, images or text in one batch, look once (review_frames on the plan, or one contact_sheet), fix only concrete defects in one more call, then go straight on to the next stage. Never re-read what you've read, never re-check unchanged work, and when a tool is still running wait for it in long blocks (in Codex, yield_time_ms 30000 or more), never calling it again just to check.

The stages, in order:
1. RESEARCH (skill research). If the project has voice-over recordings that aren't on their cut-ins yet, place them (place_voiceovers). If case_articles already lists articles about this case and the found images are weighed, don't search again: read what's saved (one call). Otherwise find, check and save the matching articles (add_article) and collect images (the people arrested, by name; the place as reported; anything the narration names), weeding them with look_at_found / reject_found. Note for later: the date and place (street, city, state) for the map, who was arrested, their exact charges as the articles word them (with the source), and each person's mugshot (its found id), or that there is none.
2. THE INTRO (skills intro, transitions): the opening reverse dip, speaker captions (caption_speakers), punch-ins and witness zooms, B-roll over its cut-ins (cover_cut_in operations, then what's particular to each), transitions with their sound. get_edit_context with section "intro" and include_transcript has what you need.
3. THE CUT-INS (skills cut-ins, transitions): every cut-in between the intro and the outro: the date cut-in's map_view (the place from research), B-roll from the footage timed to the narration's phrases, emphasis runs, introducing a person, quote callouts, transitions varied and every one heard. get_edit_context with section "story" has every cut-in's words; one find_in_footage (a phrase per idea in the narration) finds the shots for all of them; look_at_media only where the words don't settle what's on screen. Lay out all the cut-ins and their extras in one apply_edit_plan, with review_frames on each cut-in's first shot and its callouts.
4. THE OUTRO (skill outro): the charges screens from research, one per person (people charged together share one), then end the video on the outro's last word (get_edit_context section "outro" has its word timings). One apply_edit_plan with review_frames on each screen.
5. SUBTITLES (skill subtitles): watch_footage (cached by now, usually), add_subtitles after the intro (neglect: true for a child abuse or neglect case), one short label per person in one update (Edit gives each person one colour itself), then review_edit once and fix only the uncovered stretches it reports and lines that read oddly. Don't read transcripts or render frames to check speakers: the watcher looked; check only its doubtful lines.
6. MUSIC AND SOUND (skill music-and-sound): decide the case's tone, read list_library once, choose the beds (say which and why), lay them scene by scene with every transition and reveal heard, all in one batch of add_from_library calls with under_speech (it sets each level against the edit's speech: nothing to measure).
7. THE FINAL PASS (skill final-pass).

Then finish with a short summary: the edit's length, its sections, which charges you used and where they came from, and everything you weren't sure about that the user should look at.
