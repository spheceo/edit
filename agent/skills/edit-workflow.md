THE EDITING WORKFLOW: the whole edit, start to finish, in this one turn ("start the edit", "start the editing workflow"); whatever else the message says steers every stage. Don't stop to ask: decide, and keep a short list of what you weren't sure about for the summary. Watching the footage started in the background when you loaded this skill. An existing partial edit ("resume", "recover", "finish what is missing"): load resume-edit first; the numbered stages below are a checklist, not instructions to rebuild finished work.

FOOTAGE THE IMPORT COULDN'T FETCH: when get_edit_context's `import` is `waiting`, some footage couldn't be downloaded even after Edit tried again (a private or deleted video, a file nobody shared). This is the one time to stop and ask, before editing anything: name each missing piece by its label and say why in plain words, then ask "Go on without it, or do you have a replacement?" (a Drive file or YouTube link of the same recording). Their link: replace_footage. Go on without it: skip_footage. Never ask them to retry: Edit already did. While `import` says it's still importing, wait for it; edit once it's gone from the context.

Minutes, not hours: each tool call is a model round trip. Per stage: load its skill once, one get_edit_context narrowed to the stage, decide everything, one apply_edit_plan for the timeline and one batch for the tools that place things, look once (review_frames or one contact_sheet), fix only concrete defects, go on. Never re-read or re-check unchanged work; wait for slow tools in long blocks, never polling.

COMPILATIONS AND REUSE: follow the cases in the script's order, whichever kind of video. The team wants reused scripts' "footage and its cut-ins just thrown in exactly as it is": blocks noted "Reused script (preserve ranges and cut-in words)" keep the earlier script's ranges and finished narration, not a new script or a shortened summary. Do not rewrite those cut-ins or retime their source ranges automatically. A finished video explicitly marked "Reuse (the whole video)", or given by its review link (feedback.spheceo.com/r/…: the version the team approved, else the newest), plays untouched: skip every editing stage over that section, including cut-ins, subtitles and music. A finished video the user hands you mid-edit ("put this in as case 3"): insert_compilation_case with its review link, or with finished: true for a Drive or YouTube one; never as footage to edit. A reused Doc is an existing script rebuilt from its own footage, not a finished video unless one was explicitly supplied.

1. RESEARCH (skill research): generate_voiceover fills missing narration on untouched import cards and reuses saved takes, even when some cut-ins are already voiced. Preserve placed narration; place_voiceovers replaces matched takes, so use it only for recordings the user wants placed. Saved articles and weighed images: read them (one call). Else find and save the case's articles and collect images (the people arrested, the place, what the narration names). Note the date and place for the map, who was arrested, their exact charges with the source, each person's mugshot or that there is none.
2. THE INTRO (skills intro, transitions; get_edit_context section "intro", include_transcript): first its teasers (WHAT'S IN IT: with a script, its teaser ranges exactly and in its order; without one ~30 s, the strongest moments, ending on the arrest; audit intro_long), then their treatment.
3. THE CUT-INS (skills cut-ins, transitions; section "story"): the date cut-in's map, B-roll timed to the narration (one find_in_footage for all of them), people introduced, quotes, every transition heard; one apply_edit_plan with review_frames on each cut-in's first shot.
4. THE OUTRO (skill outro; section "outro").
5. SUBTITLES (skill subtitles): watch_footage (cached by now), add_subtitles, one short label per person in one update, review_edit once for uncovered stretches; check only the watcher's doubtful lines.
6. MUSIC AND SOUND (skill music-and-sound): the case's tone, list_library once, the beds scene by scene with every transition heard, one batch of add_from_library with under_speech.
7. THE FINAL PASS (skill final-pass).

Finish with a short summary: the length, the sections, the charges used and their sources, and what the user should look at.
