---
description: The intro: the opening dip, speaker captions, punch-ins, B-roll over its cut-ins.
---

- The INTRO is the hook: the short teaser clips from the start up to the "On <date>" cut-in that starts the story (without one, the cut-in that leads into the full footage). It can have cut-ins between its teasers (they're part of it, but get none of the footage treatment): one cut-in makes a two-split intro, two a three-split, and so on. Each teaser ends on a finished sentence, never mid-phrase (see the footage-range rule below), and has no dead air: a pause between its lines is cut out (remove_time; audit dead_air: "dead space like this must be removed"). It's where the treatment goes:
  - It always opens with a reverse dip to black: add_clip {type "solid", fill [0,0,0,1], start 0, duration about 0.5 s in frames, track "new"} so it covers everything, then set_keyframes opacity [{frame 0, value 1}, {frame duration-1, value 0}].
  - A TRANSITION on each cut into and out of a cut-in (see TRANSITIONS: varied, and heard): a flash or shutter, a short dip, a crossfade. Never the vertical drop (the shake preset "vertical") unless the user asks for it: the team isn't a fan of it ("not a fan of this transition right here", on a drop into the intro's first cut-in).
  - Speaker captions on what's said (never with a name label above them: "Officer:" labels are for the subtitles after the intro only): run caption_speakers (it works out who speaks each line by watching and listening, then places word-by-word captions in the house box style by that person (a see-through black box, background opacity 0.45, so the footage shows through, with a wide soft shadow: shadow size 9, blur 14 at 1080p; subtitles keep their opaque box), with a little shake). WHERE A CAPTION GOES: under the SPEAKER's face only when that face is clearly visible through the line (found by the face detector, the right person, close enough to read), following it smoothly; otherwise bottom middle. Never beside or under anyone else, never on any face, never pinned to a hand, an object or an empty spot. Edit does this by itself (caption_speakers; place_captions re-lays captions already made, without retyping them). Review the placements together in the stage contact sheet: each caption is under the person saying it or bottom middle; for one under the wrong person, run place_captions with under {"<clip id>": [x, y] a point on the speaker's face at its first frame} or "bottom" (SPEAKERS for who says what). Never key a caption's x/y by hand or with track_region.
  - Punch-ins on reactions and key lines: scale keyframes from 1 to about 1.08–1.15 over a few frames, centred on the face (move x/y too). A witness or other key speaker telling what happened gets a slow zoom in on them across their line (scale 1 to ~1.1, ease linear, centred on their face) so the eye stays on them.
  - B-roll over cuts: while someone keeps talking, lay a different shot (another moment of the footage, the scene, the other person reacting) on a video track above for 1–3 s as picture only; the sound underneath carries on. Prefer moments the transcript or the Doc's notes point to.
  - Quick white flashes or dips on hard scene changes, sparingly.
- ONE WORD AT A TIME: a short shouted line that's the moment itself ("Shots fired!", "Drop it!", "Stop!", "Gun!") reads harder with each word alone: set that caption's animation preset to "word" (same word_times), in the intro captions and the subtitles alike. A few per video, on the peaks; everyday talk stays word by word.
