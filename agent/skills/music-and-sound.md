- PLAN FIRST (the team's Cut-In Guide): break the whole video down before choosing anything: what are we hearing, and what's the vibe at each point? Then decide the case's tone and tell the user which you chose and why (list_library once, then add_from_library). A killing or a chase: dark thriller beds (drones, pulses, tension). A neglect or family abuse case: serious, restrained beds with a steady pulse, never murder-thriller tension or melodrama ("Titanic theme music" was sent back): sad is carried by the footage. A lighter case (an entitled driver, an absurd arrest): energy and play, never horror drones. If nothing in the library fits, say so.
- MOVE WITH THE STORY: a bed fits the scene it plays under, not just the case, and changes at natural points (a revelation, a new subject, each cut-in), starting where its music starts, never mid-phrase. A SONG IS GOOD FOR A SHORT STRETCH (usually its first 10–20 s, or the part a note names): one bed per scene, never one song running a minute (audit music_bed_long) and never the same song restarted under cut-in after cut-in (Mom Leaves 5 Kids: the team asked to change the music on nearly every one; audit music_repeated). Under the footage too, either barely noticed or tied to the setting (eerie at a crime scene), never loud. Under a cut-in the music is part of the sound design, "like it was always there", never a new track that turns upbeat.
- A BED NEVER STOPS UNDER THE NARRATION: it runs to its cut-in's end and fades (~1.5 s) or hands over ("the music stops all of a sudden"; audit music_stops_mid_narration). The intro's teasers and its first cut-in get different beds. The last bed fades out by the video's last word.
- THE FOOTAGE IS AS LOUD AS THE NARRATION: what's said on screen sits at the voice-overs' level, never a quiet room under a loud narrator (the Sabrina Limon interrogation played 15 LU under its voice-overs: "increase the audio of the footage to try and have it match the sort of average volume for the voiceover"). Placing voice-overs levels footage still at 0 dB; after recutting or adding footage, level_footage once (audit footage_quiet / footage_loud).
- LEVELS with add_from_library's under_speech (LU under the edit's speech, measured for you; never a guessed volume_db): music ~14 under speech (~6 with nothing said over it); a hit on a drop or reveal about as loud as the speech; whooshes and shutters 3–4 under that; risers ~8 under; small clicks ~8 under. measure_audio only when the user says the mix sounds wrong.
- MARK, THEN PLACE: mark what happens on screen in one markers call (footage moments on their media, graphic moments on the timeline), then place each sound on its marks (markers: [name]) in the one batch of sounds. Evidence boards lay their own shutters; the Windows screen marks "desktop window opens" (a subtle click each: two or three near-identical clicks taken in turn, so a run never sounds mechanical) and "desktop line" (a soft typewriter under each note). A sound matches how a thing happens: a click for what pops on, nothing for a fade.
- EVERY TRANSITION IS HEARD (audit silent_transition): a riser building into a new scene, place or time (starting ~0.5–1.5 s before the cut, peaking on it), a hit on landing; into a person speaking, a soft hit or the music carries it. WHOOSHES are an accent: with a dip or a real change of scene, never two within ~10 s. Into and out of a cut-in, something makes the move natural: a click as the frame pauses, a riser on the way out into a strong moment, a wind texture, or a second or two of SILENCE before footage that starts slow and fresh (officers stepping into the room for the first time): "silence resets the viewer's ear".
