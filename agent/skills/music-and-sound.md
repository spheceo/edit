description: Music beds and sound effects from the team's library, and their levels.

- PLAN FIRST: what do we hear at each point, and what's its vibe? Choose the case's tone once (list_library once, then add_from_library) and say why: a killing or a chase gets dark thriller beds; neglect or family abuse serious, restrained beds with a steady pulse, never thriller tension or melodrama ("Titanic theme music" was sent back); a lighter case energy and play, never horror drones. Read list_library's notes for each track's feel.
- A BED FITS ITS SCENE and changes only where what we hear changes (a cut-in starting or ending, the footage back, a new case, a revelation, the outro), on a cut and heard (a riser or hit), never mid-narration ("we only need to change the music if there is some switch in what we hear"). A song is good for a short stretch, usually its first 10–20 s: never one song running a minute, never the same song under cut-in after cut-in (audits music_bed_long, music_repeated, music_change_mid_narration). Facts, suspicion, a lie or a reveal want tension that moves; slow, sad pads only where the story is grief ("this sounds like funeral music"). A bed never stops under the narration: it runs to its cut-in's end and fades (~1.5 s) (music_stops_mid_narration). The last bed fades out by the last word.
- LEVELS with add_from_library's under_speech (never a guessed volume_db): music ~20 LU under the narration or speech ("we need to turn down the music", asked again at 16: "significantly"; audit music_loud), ~6 with nothing said over it; a hit on a reveal about as loud as the speech; whooshes and shutters 3–4 under; risers ~8 under; clicks ~8 under. The footage is as loud as the narration (level_footage after recutting; footage_quiet, footage_loud).
- MARK, THEN PLACE: one markers call for what happens on screen, then every sound on its marks in one batch. Every picture that pops on gets a click (two or three near-identical clicks in turn), a Notepad line a soft typewriter; a fade gets nothing.
- EVERY TRANSITION IS HEARD (audit silent_transition): a riser building into a new scene, place or time (from ~0.5–1.5 s before, peaking on the cut), a hit on landing; whooshes only with a real change of scene, never two within ~10 s. A second or two of silence before footage that starts fresh resets the ear.
