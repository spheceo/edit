description: Music beds and sound effects from the team's library, and their levels.
---

- MUSIC AND SOUND EFFECTS come from the team's library (list_library, then add_from_library), when the user asks for them. They set the video's vibe, so choose it deliberately before placing anything: read the script and footage, decide the case's tone and tell the user which you chose and why. A murder or really dark case (death, abuse, a disturbing scene) gets the dark beds: drones, low tones, tension; but match the kind of dark: thriller music (pulses, stabs, a hunt) is for a killing or a chase, while a neglect or family abuse case (a child left alone, a filthy home) wants serious, restrained beds with a steady pulse that keep the story moving, never murder-thriller tension and never melodrama: no swelling orchestral or piano ballads, nothing a viewer would hear as a film's tearjerker theme (the team rejected one as "Titanic theme music" and asked for something slightly more upbeat). Sad is carried by the footage, not by the music. Keep one tone across the video. A lighter or upbeat case (an entitled driver, an absurd arrest, someone getting their comeuppance) gets music with energy and play and lighter sound design, never horror drones, eerie beds or heartbeats. Move the music with the story (tension into a build-up, revelation at the reveal), never at random. Each bed must fit the scene it plays under, not just the case: read its notes against what's on screen there (officers arriving on a fatal crash is grim and tense, not a chase or heist), and change beds at scene changes and cut-ins, starting the new one where the music starts (its beginning or the part a note names), never mid-phrase. A SONG IS GOOD FOR A SHORT STRETCH: usually only its opening (often just the first 10–20 s) matches the vibe, and later in the song it turns into something unusable; so a bed plays only that stretch (or the part a note names) and the next scene gets another bed, never one song running on for a minute (the audit flags music_bed_long past 25 s). MANY BEDS, NONE REPEATED: each cut-in (and each scene of the intro) gets its own bed of the case's tone; never the same song restarted from its first second under cut-in after cut-in (Mom Leaves 5 Kids played one song under ten cut-ins and another under four, and the team asked to change the music on nearly every one; the audit flags music_repeated: a song in three places or more). A song may come back once, far from where it was. THE INTRO changes music at its first cut-in: the teasers' bed stops with them and the narration's first cut-in starts its own (one song under the whole hook and its narration was sent back). A BED NEVER STOPS UNDER THE NARRATION: it runs to its cut-in's last frame and fades out there (~1.5 s), or hands over to the next bed; a bed ending while the voice-over carries on sounds like a mistake ("the music stops all of a sudden"; the audit flags music_stops_mid_narration). If nothing in the library fits the tone, say so rather than forcing a track that doesn't. Change a bed where its tone doesn't fit the case or the scene, where it has played its good stretch, and at each cut-in (above). Pick by the library's notes and usage guide (a shutter on crime scene images, a hit on a reveal, mugshot or chapter title, a riser building into a reveal or hard cut). Play only the part a note names ("first ~20 seconds", via seconds) and fade music out over ~1.5 s when it's cut short. The last bed fades out by the end of the outro's voice-over, where the video ends (THE OUTRO); never past it. LEVELS: set them with add_from_library's under_speech (LU under the edit's speech, which it measures itself), never a guessed volume_db and never measuring first. Music sits about 14 LU under the speech it plays beneath (voice-over or footage; about 16 under where the note says "low volume") and never competes with it; with nothing said over it, it can rise to about 6 under the speech around it. A sound marks something that happens on screen and matches how it happens: a click for something that pops on, never for a fade (the place label fades in: no click). Accents must be clearly heard: a hit or stinger on a drop or reveal about as loud as the speech around it, whooshes and shutters about 3–4 below that, small clicks about 8 below. RISERS sit well under the voice, about 8 LU under the speech they play beneath (a swell felt under the words, never on top of them). The levels below keep every sound heard without drowning a word; measure_audio is only for a mix the user says sounds wrong.
- MARK, THEN PLACE: before laying sound effects on what happens in the picture (a door slam, a camera flash, a photo landing, a window opening), mark those moments in one markers call (a moment of the footage on its media: {name, media, time} or {name, clip, frame}, so it stays on the moment if clips move; a moment of a graphic on the timeline: {name, frame}), then place each sound on its marks (add_from_library / add_sound_effect markers: [name]) in the one batch of sounds. get_edit_context lists the marks. Evidence boards mark their photos ("board photo") and lay their own shutters; desktop profiles mark "desktop window opens" (a soft UI click each) and "desktop line".
- EVERY TRANSITION IS HEARD, and a scene change is felt in the sound before it's seen: a riser building into the cut (starting ~0.5–1.5 s before it, peaking on the cut), a hit or impact on landing for a hard cut. Risers build into a new scene, place or time; not into a cut to a person speaking (the suspect's first clip, a witness): there a soft hit or the music carries it. WHOOSHES are an accent, not a habit: one goes with a dip to black or a real change of scene, never on a plain cut or a crossfade between B-roll shots (the music carries those; a cut on a phrase needs no sound), and never two within ~10 s of each other, or the edit turns into whoosh after whoosh. Into a new scene (a new place or time: the date cut-in, the story starting) the music moves with it: the outgoing bed fades out or drops away under the riser, and the new one starts on the cut, so the change never comes as a bare hard cut from one scene's sound to the next. The plan's audit flags a drop, flash or dip with nothing heard on it (silent_transition).
