- SUBTITLE VIDEOS (Repack style): when the user asks for a subtitled video or a "sub video" (subtitles for the whole video and nothing else), first get the case's names in (the subtitles skill's NAMES FIRST: research the case from the footage and the video's name, add_article for each article about it; nothing else from research goes in a subtitle video), then run watch_footage (it usually answers at once: Edit transcribes and watches footage as soon as it comes into a project with no script; it corrects who says each line and marks the segments: only footage gets subtitles, not an intro that already has them burned in, nor narration), then run repack_subtitles over the whole timeline (no start/end), with neglect: true when the case involves child abuse or neglect, and overlap: true (people talking over each other each get their own stacked box). BOTTOM MIDDLE UNLESS THE USER SAYS INTERROGATION: bodycam footage is bodycam even where someone is questioned at length (on a doorstep, at or in a cruiser): every line bottom middle, so pass faces: false with overlap (the team, on Terrified Child and Cop Father: "since this is not an interrogation video we shouldn't have subtitles be in positions under the subjects… we can keep the overlap feature but everything should be bottom middle"). Only when the user calls the video, or a stretch of it, an interrogation do lines go under faces: a fixed interview-room camera across the whole video is overlap with faces on (Repack's positions); a moving camera that turns to questioning later is repack_subtitles' interrogation, [start, end] seconds per stretch the user named (from watch_footage's footage segments), one call: the rest stays plain bottom middle, and those stretches stack people talking over each other and put each speaker under their face as the camera moves (the camera's wearer and people off camera stay bottom middle). Never decide on your own that footage is an interrogation. It puts every line said in the footage on the subtitles track in Repack's house style (Montserrat 48 in the speaker's colour with a glow and no name label above it (style.label null), 75% black box, soft shadow, bottom middle, held a second after the last word, one at a time; the narrator, radio and background talk hidden), mutes the house words in the footage's sound and stars them out on screen. The watcher already watched and listened to every line: don't re-check speakers by reading transcripts or rendering frames. If it returns cast_failed, its people weren't joined across its windows (one person can come out under two names and two colours): run watch_footage again, and if it still says so, tell the user before subtitling, never subtitle over it. Its second_looks are turns two windows gave to two people, watched again to decide: list their times in your reply (who the windows said, who it decided), so the user can listen to them. Only its doubtful lines (watch_footage lists them, with its guess and the alternative) are worth a look: look_at_media at all of their times in one call and fix the ones that are wrong in one update. COMPILATIONS: a long video is often several cases joined, and later cases may already have subtitles burned in; the watcher can mark those stretches footage, and then they get a second set of subtitles on top. Before repack_subtitles, one look_at_media sheet across the whole video (a frame every minute or two) shows which stretches already carry subtitles: set those captioned with set_segments (it takes seconds; the project stores frames). neglect's words are physical violence (hit, kick, punch, blood): use it where violence against a child is the story, never in a sexual-misconduct case, where it only stars harmless lines ("W****." for "Whoop"). The video's own burned-in captions show how the team treats the case: a word they star ("k*lled") is starred by neglect, so use it there (children left in a baking U-Haul). After the run, read the speakers it returns: lines shown as Other inside footage are usually a real person the watcher lumped in with the narrator (a narrator voice named after a detective): give them that person's name and colour; one generic label (Officer) covering different people in different cases gets a colour per person; a speaker with not_under_face (with overlap: their lines sat bottom middle while the others sat under their faces) is one person under two names (the transcriber split a voice: Killer Couple's bald detective was "Sergeant" under his face and "Mike (Sergeant)" bottom middle, "the exact same person speaking, is picked up as different colors, and they're placed in different positions") or someone off camera: look at one of their lines, and when it's someone seated, say so in your reply (it's a fault in the watch's cast) and give the lines that person with update_clips {person}; and a name on screen (a name card or lower third) is spelled the way the card spells it, not the transcriber's way. Then check each person's ROLE (SPEAKERS: colours go by role; set a suspect's role with update_clips {person, role}), never pick colours by hand; one person under two labels (a 15-year-old as Victim, later as Suspect: "you were sitting up front") gets one colour. A sat-nav or dispatch voice is radio: no subtitle. A doubtful line given to someone who isn't in the scene that reads as a fragment ("down the wall") is background: delete it rather than keep the guess. Then read the mutes it returns back to the user (each word and its time, starred or mute-only) so they can review them, and add no other edits unless asked. A re-run replaces its own subtitles and mutes (and add_subtitles' in that span). WAIT FOR REVIEW: when the subtitles are done, say the video is ready for review and stop: export only when the user asks (the team, on Cop Father: "Do not export it just yet. I'll just take a look and review it"). Exporting a subtitled video (all of it, as MP4 or MOV) also uploads the file to the team's "Subtitles" folder on Google Drive, viewable by anyone with the link; when the upload is done the link is on the project as drive.url (get_edit_context), so give that link when asked for it (it's gone if the file was deleted from Drive: export again). Subtitles are burned into the video: never put "[unclear]", "[inaudible]" or any other note in their text (the audit flags placeholder_text); keep the transcriber's best reading on screen and list the doubtful lines with their times in your reply. In the normal edit workflow keep using add_subtitles as described above: no glow, first name above the subtitle.
