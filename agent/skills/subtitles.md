description: Subtitles in an edit (after the intro): watching, wording, speakers and their colours.
---

  - It does get plain subtitles throughout (Repack's subtitles), as part of editing the whole video: first run watch_footage (it corrects who says each line and marks background talk; on an edit it leaves the segments alone), then add_subtitles (it defaults to everything after the intro), with neglect: true when the case involves child abuse or neglect. The narrator, radio and background talk get no subtitles; the house words are muted in the footage's sound and the narration's (voice-overs) and starred out on screen (read the mutes it returns back to the user). They sit bottom middle on the subtitles track, except over a face: A SUBTITLE NEVER COVERS A FACE, above all the speaker's (bodycam often looks down at people, faces low in the frame). add_subtitles moves any subtitle a face would be under, by itself, to the first clear place (bottom left or right, else the top), from the faces it finds in the footage, and keeps a conversation's lines together; place_subtitles does the same for subtitles made before (it changes nothing else). Leave those where Edit put them. A blur or redaction box is not a face: overlapping one is fine, and never move a subtitle up or to the side to dodge one; any moved off bottom middle by hand that way go back (update_clip transform {x 0, y 0.4*height}; set_keyframes [] on any x/y they were given) and then run place_subtitles, since add_subtitles keeps hand-moved positions. No animation, no tracking: white words without glow in a pure black box (opaque, unlike the intro captions' see-through 0.45 box), the speaker's name above in their colour ("Officer:"). Timing matters most: each line is on screen while it's said, no earlier or later. Where a line repeats ("I'm hungry" … "I'm hungry again"), check each one against the transcript's word times (read_transcript) and in the stage contact sheet at those moments, so each appears when it's said and one never stands in for both; the same for the intro's captions. Short lines and lines that read oddly are where the transcriber slips (a word that makes no sense there, a phrase missing a word: "hit me in the" without "head", a line opening on a word that ends the one before): check them against the word times and what's said around them, fix the text or move the word to its line when the context makes it clear, and list the ones you couldn't settle for the user. EVERY LINE SAID IN THE FOOTAGE after the intro gets a subtitle: once they're in, run review_edit for cached transcript coverage and use its uncovered speech timecodes instead of comparing the entire transcript and subtitle track by hand (a file not transcribed, a voice wrongly marked background, a range added later); fix the cause and run add_subtitles over that stretch. The intro keeps its own speaker captions (caption_speakers), glow and all. If footage after the intro changes (a range realigned, a clip moved), run add_subtitles again; it replaces its own and their mutes, keeping where and how big subtitles moved by hand were. Subtitles finished by hand that only need the house words muted and starred: mute_house_words (it moves nothing).

- SUBTITLES STAY IN THEIR SHOT: a subtitle never runs past the cut it's said before: not into a cut-in (card, B-roll or voice-over) and not into the next shot, even by a frame; it ends at that cut when its hold would carry it over (the audit flags subtitle_past_cut). And each word shows when it's said: a line whose last word lands after it's heard ("head" a beat late) is retimed from read_transcript's word times.
- SUBTITLE WORDING: follow Repack: keep the transcriber's words when wording is uncertain. Apply a correction only when the evidence settles it; otherwise retain the transcriber's best reading and report the uncertainty with its timecode separately to the user. Never replace speech with "[unclear]", "[inaudible]" or another review placeholder on screen, or drop words just because you cannot settle them.
- SPEAKERS: one name and one colour per person for the whole video (two people never share a colour). Subtitles take their labels and colours from each file's transcript, so one person can come out under different names or colours in different files, or drift between roles (Officer, then Sergeant, then Medic). For normal edit projects only, after the last add_subtitles run, list the labels in use (get_edit_context speaker_line_counts: each label's lines, colour and the transcript voices it covers), work out who is who across files, and give each person one short label: one update_clips {label: "<the label as it is>", patch: {style: {label: {text}}}} per label to rename or merge, which changes every subtitle carrying it at once (never update_clip one subtitle at a time). A label IS a person: different people keep different labels (three sisters are "Nevaeh:", "Sister:" and "Sister 2:" until you know their names, never all "Sister:"; a named officer is "Sinclair:", not one more "Officer:"; "Child", "Child 2" and "Child 3" are three children), and one person never has two. Merge two transcript voices under one label only when watch_footage (most of its windows, not one) or the footage shows they're the same person; Edit lists every named voice under someone else's label (other_voices_under_this_label after a relabel) so each merge is one you meant. COLOURS GO BY ROLE, the team's code for every subtitle and caption, in subtitled videos and edits alike (their subtitle guide): officers teal #00E5C7; sergeants, detectives and whoever leads the police side blue #0090E5; ALL SUSPECTS yellow #FFFF00 (yellow is for nobody else: a doctor in yellow left the team asking "why is this yellow? I think this is a medical person"); victims and witnesses orange #E57E00 (a witness white when a victim is in it too); paramedics, doctors, nurses, firefighters, DCF and social workers purple #F33BFF; a brief bystander white; anyone else a light extra (green, light pink, lime); two people of one role get shades of it, never dark tones. Edit colours each person (label) by their role after add_subtitles, caption_speakers, update_clips and every plan (their subtitle labels, intro captions and quotes named "Quote · <label>" alike) and keeps people near in time apart; you never set colours. Your part is who is who and their ROLE IN THE STORY: Edit reads it from the label ("Sergeant", "Doctor"), but a mother, father or neighbour the story makes the suspect is a suspect: update_clips {person: "<label>", role: "suspect"} (roles: officer, leader, suspect, victim, witness, medic, bystander, other), and their colour follows. Set roles before the subtitles stage ends; check each named person's role against the case (who is accused, who was hurt). Subtitle projects never get name labels: use clip names and transcript speakers to reconcile people, and update style.color for their subtitle words while keeping style.label null and glow enabled. Normal edit projects have glow disabled and a first-name label above the subtitle when the name is known (otherwise a short role); the intro's speaker captions never get a label (relabelling a speaker leaves them as they are). Short labels: first names for family and civilians ("Pearl:", "Latricia:", "Benjamin:", never "Latricia Smith (Mother)"); officers by first name when known too, otherwise the known name or a short role ("Officer:" when unnamed); a minor victim by first name or "Victim:", never "Teenager:" or "15-year-old victim:" (age and role belong only on the callout that introduces them). Once someone is named they keep the name: never back to "Suspect:" or "Man:". Who speaks: the bodycam's wearer (the POV; the Axon or department tag in a corner says whose camera it is) is police, never a medic or a witness, and theirs is the nearest, loudest voice; a voice that isn't the wearer's is someone else in the scene. When a label looks doubtful (a role that doesn't fit, a name that changes a line later, "Officer" right after a named officer in the same conversation), check by watching, never by guessing: watch_footage first (it watched and listened), then look_at_media at the line only if it's still unclear (whose mouth moves, who's in the shot, who's on the phone) and the transcript around it; the same voice in the same scene is the same person, so give them the same label.
- ONE WORD AT A TIME: a short shouted line that's the moment itself ("Shots fired!", "Drop it!", "Stop!", "Gun!") reads harder with each word alone: set that caption's animation preset to "word" (same word_times), in the intro captions and the subtitles alike. A few per video, on the peaks; everyday talk stays word by word.
