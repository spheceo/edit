- THE SUBTITLES (Repack's, after the intro; the intro keeps its own speaker captions): watch_footage first (who says each line, what's background), then add_subtitles (everything after the intro by default; neglect: true in a child abuse or neglect case). The narrator, radio and background talk get none; house words are muted and starred (read the mutes back). White words, no glow, in a pure black box, the speaker's name above in their colour ("Officer:"), no animation, LOCKED BOTTOM MIDDLE whatever is under them, a face included ("They must be locked to the bottom middle"; audit subtitle_moved, place_subtitles puts them back).
- TIMING: each line on screen while it's said, never past the cut it's said before (subtitle_past_cut). A line that repeats ("I'm hungry" … "I'm hungry again"), a short line or one that reads oddly is where the transcriber slips: check it against read_transcript's word times and the words around it, fix it when the context settles it, else keep the transcriber's best reading (never "[unclear]") and list it with its time for the user.
- EVERY LINE SAID after the intro gets one: review_edit's uncovered speech says where not; fix the cause and add_subtitles over that stretch. Footage changed later (a range realigned): add_subtitles again (it keeps hand-moved positions).

- SPEAKERS: one short label and one colour per person for the whole video. After the last add_subtitles, read speaker_line_counts and work out who is who across files; rename or merge with one update_clips {label, patch: {style: {label: {text}}}} per label. A label IS a person: different people keep different labels ("Sister:", "Sister 2:"; a named officer "Sinclair:", not one more "Officer:"), one person never two; merge two voices only when watch_footage or the footage shows they're the same person. Short labels: first names ("Pearl:", never "Latricia Smith (Mother)"), officers by first name when known, else a short role; a minor victim by first name or "Victim:"; once named, always named. The bodycam's wearer is police and the nearest, loudest voice. A doubtful label: watch_footage, then look_at_media at that line, never a guess.
- COLOURS GO BY ROLE and Edit sets them (officers teal, leaders blue, a woman officer or sergeant pink when her label says so ("Female Sergeant:"; "make her pink"), ALL SUSPECTS yellow and nobody else ("why is this yellow? I think this is a medical person"), victims and witnesses orange, medics purple, bystanders white). Your part is each person's ROLE IN THE STORY: a mother the story makes the suspect is a suspect: update_clips {person, role: "suspect"} before the subtitles stage ends.
- ONE WORD AT A TIME for a short shouted line that's the moment itself ("Shots fired!", "Drop it!"): animation preset "word". A few a video.
