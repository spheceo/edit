A COMMENTARY VIDEO is a narrated investigative essay. Follow the imported Writer script, its chapter order, source excerpts and anchored production notes. The narrator carries the story; a source clip plays with its own sound where hearing it matters. These rules replace the police/court templates: no arrest teaser requirement, maps, Windows desktop, charges, sentencing, blanket captions or compulsory flash between narration paragraphs.

“B-roll must be relevant to what is being said”: show the actual people, event, period and action the line names. Prefer their own uploads, primary interviews and original posts. A title or transcript match is a lead, not proof of the picture. Inspect candidate frames, record what they show, then use distinct scenes. Don't loop one talking head through an essay, invent a missing tweet, fabricate evidence, or call a generic image an actual event. A sourced portrait can identify someone while the narrator explains an unpictured claim; it cannot substantiate the claim. Keep a short list of unresolved source requests.

The visual language is full-frame footage of the actual people and moments, hard punch-ins on the words that land, selective editorial text (Akwe Pro Bold), white framed evidence (articles, screenshots, posts with red highlights; never a person's portrait as a card), YouTube references as the channel page shows them, corner labels for a comic beat, the channel's real light leak on turns, the channel bumper in the intro, restrained grain/vignette on narration visuals, motivated camera moves, strong ease-out slides and scale pops, soft character opacity sweeps with the whole phrase already laid out. Use commentary_* plan operations. Their motion is shared between preview and export. Text says the key phrase, not every narrated word. A genuine list is left aligned; a centred phrase stays centred as it reveals. Source footage stays clean. Image/article cards need breathing room; never make an entire minute a single card.

“Never music over clips”: music only under narration, ending at every source excerpt and resuming with the narrator. Level source speech to the VO; use measured LUFS with headroom, then inspect the heard mix. The guide's -6 dB VO, -23–25 dB music and -8–12 dB SFX are relative mixer/peak guidance, not LUFS targets. Keep beds low, change on story turns, and align the loudest whoosh transient with the image's arrival. Source audio is off for B-roll under narration. No bleep: precisely silence the house words in both VO and clips (mute_house_words includes this style's extra terms), retaining first letter + stars in on-screen text. Track localized censorship of injuries, blood, real weapons and drugs; fire blurs the whole image but remains recognizable. Alcohol, cigarettes and vapes need no blur. Screenshot evidence also needs sensitive words obscured.

MrBeast's uppercase Mazzard H Bold boxed subtitles, ~80% inset source clips and brief motion B-roll are conditional on a MrBeast episode. This style does not impose them on other subjects. Credits describe real source metadata, never a promise about copyright.
