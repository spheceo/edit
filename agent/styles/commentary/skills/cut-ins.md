description: Cover commentary chapter narration with relevant footage and smooth editorial graphics.
---

Every narration span needs a picture chosen for its meaning. cover_cut_in can place clean footage shots, but explicitly set look false and into none for commentary, then use commentary_look only on the narration span. Its generic picture/desktop mode belongs to other styles: use commentary_card for a still, screenshot or inset video instead. Follow the narration's actual word times. Aim for a new meaningful picture or composition around every 3–6 seconds, allowing longer evidence reading and deliberate slow pushes. Don't cut to an unrelated shot just to satisfy a timer. A long source excerpt (>20s) needs motivated 120% punch-ins/out or gentle moves, not a decorative transition across each sentence. Remove source silences over 1.5s without clipping words.

commentary_card {media,start,end,source_in?,background?,label?,x?,y?,width?,height?,backdrop?}: clean rounded, glowing white framed picture with an ease-out slide and subtle drift, sharp over a dark blurred copy or brand wallpaper. width/height are maximum shares of the canvas (.82/.77); x/y project pixels from centre. backdrop:false adds a second card over an existing composition. Label real source metadata only. commentary_text {text,start,end,motion:soft|slide|pop|typewriter,blur?,size?,x?,y?,box?,align?}: selective editorial phrase, a chapter's few key lines, never every sentence; it sits on the B-roll, which stays seen (blur:true dims and blurs the picture behind it: rare, at most a quarter of the narration); soft is a character opacity sweep with stable full-phrase geometry, slide/pop move the complete phrase. For a person+list composition use commentary_profile {media,name?,background?,start,end,lines:[{text,at}]}: a portrait left, short fact bullets right, at in absolute frames as each fact is said. Its complete final list keeps the left edge stable as it reveals. Do not reveal the entire list before it is said. A screenshot is real evidence, cropped to the relevant region; highlight only its actual words, never redraw its content as if original.

Choose each beat's composition from the reference's whole vocabulary, never the same two on repeat: "we use background blur, a box in the middle… The second one is just text in the middle, and that's it." Full-frame footage carries most lines. commentary_punch cuts closer on the words that land and holds, no animated zoom, sometimes twice or three times in a row for drama: "It is chosen so carefully based on what is being said and done on screen." Always into the person being talked about (x/y where they are; "N3on is on the bottom-left corner. It should be punching into him"), and mostly on a source clip where someone is talking; on B-roll under the narrator only for its sharpest lines ("when we're saying something… we're just overdoing it"). What the team loved: on a wide shot of him and his game, "as we talk more weirdly about him, we sort of cut in and move a lot closer to him". commentary_card is for evidence (an article, a screenshot, a document), not people: a person is shown in footage of them ("I'm not sure about us using this sort of picture style for showing images of these people"); never a thumbnail with its own big title text. Every screenshot of YouTube or X becomes our own graphic: "we need to try and have as many graphics that are based on screenshots as possible… If we can't get it from the API, we take a screenshot and fill in the information." A video talked about is commentary_youtube (youtube_reference, else the details read off the screenshot and its frame from still_frame crop), several uploads commentary_youtube_list, a post commentary_tweet (tweet_reference, else read off the screenshot) with highlight on the phrase the line is about, not the whole post. Never show a phone recording or an old screenshot of a page as it was. The editorial line goes in separate text below. A comic beat can play a short reaction at speed 2 with a commentary_label stating the obvious ("*COMPLETE CRASHOUT*": "Tiny things like this can go a very long way"); that's rare, a few an essay, and never the way to emphasise a line: big emphasis is commentary_text in the middle ("have the sort of drop-shadow big text be in the middle instead"). A card lasts at least 2.5 s, and consecutive graphics butt together: a gap lets the footage under them flash up for a few frames ("We cannot have mistakes like that"). Source excerpts start and end on their quote's words (realign_script) and are cut word to word: tighten_voiceover footage:true takes out the gaps unless a pause is the point.

After composing each chapter, check_shots over it (two minutes at a time) and replace every shot whose picture doesn't show what its words name; a caption, title or graphic that says something else is wrong too ("it mentions a Houston highlights thing. There is no Houston highlights anything here"). What good looks like, in the team's words: the run from Kanye West to Megan Fox, each person named shown in real footage with him at the word that names them.

commentary_chapter {text,number,start,end} is an episode-neutral chapter slate, ideally over a chapter turn already in the narration rather than an extra silence. Chapter names follow the Writer headings. Brand wallpaper or a real folder asset can replace its background; never retain another episode's baked title. commentary_look {start,end} is slight grain/vignette and belongs only on narration visuals. Avoid stacking it with another grade. Hard cuts, punch-ins and smooth compositional changes do most of the work; commentary_leak on a turn, no perpetual flash machine. End graphics and blur with their own shot.
