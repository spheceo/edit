- A COURTROOM VIDEO is a hearing (a trial, a sentencing, a bond, motion or discipline hearing; a court livestream or a Zoom/Teams recording) told as a story: the hearing is the footage and carries the drama; the narration only explains it. Its shape: the cold open (the teasers: the intro), the hook cut-in, the setup cut-in ("On <date>…": who, what they did, what's at stake), then the hearing in long stretches (1–3 minutes each) with a short narration bridge (a cut-in of 10–20 s) between them, and the outcome at the end (the outro: THE SENTENCING CARD). Inside a range cut only dead air (paperwork, long silences, a recess) with a hard cut and no sound, never a speaker's sentence.
- VOICE-OVERS: the import voices the cut-ins with this style's courtroom voice (`vVj9i7yXuhHiV9JHF0kj`), through `ELEVENLABS_VOICES_KEY` ("<project> (voice-over) - Cut-in N"), so the edit can be built at once; a recording the user adds later takes its place. When the cut-ins have no voice-over (an import that couldn't make one: on Judge EXPLODES the team "just couldn't generate a voiceover"), generate_voiceover, and never wait for a recording or ask for one. RECORDED ONES: before placing, read each recording's transcript (media_transcript) against the script's cut-ins: only a recording that reads THIS script goes on (one of another case, or an older take of this one, stays off the timeline: say which and why); of several takes of this script, the newest the user names, else the one that matches the script best. place_voiceovers {media_ids: [it]}; it places only the words that read each cut-in, so anything else in the file (a note to the editor read out, a retake, a count-in) stays off: check the last cut-in ends on its last scripted word. Where the recording words a line differently from the Doc, the recording wins (it's what's heard): text on screen follows it. Voice-overs Edit generated ("<project> (voice-over) - Cut-in N") are replaced by the recording (place_voiceovers with the recording's id). With no recording, the generated one stays.
- THE HEARING'S PICTURE: AFTER THE INTRO doesn't hold here; court cameras are fixed, so the edit makes the moves. Hard cuts between angles, never a transition. A PUNCH-IN (scale ~1.3–2 on one person or one pane of a grid, x/y on them, held still, no animation) on the lines that land (a ruling, a rebuke, an admission, "Is that clear?") and on a person's first lines; back out when the exchange widens. A grid of feeds (panes side by side or stacked, black bars around them) fills the frame: scale it up past the bars; where one pane holds the speaker for a stretch, punch into that pane. Another channel's logo, banners, "coming up" cards or captions burned into a re-upload are never on screen: crop past them (scale and x/y), else a rect blur over them (MINORS' FACES); the court's own name tags and seals can stay. Clerk monitors and papers with personal details get a rect blur.
- NARRATION BRIDGES (every cut-in after the setup) are covered with the hearing itself, as B-roll: cover_cut_in with into "none", 2–4 shots from around that point (never ahead of the story): whom the narration names, punched in, then whoever reacts (the judge leaning back, an attorney's face); the B-roll look stays. EACH SHOT IS ONE PICTURE: a court stream switches views mid-hearing (a document, another camera, a grid), so a shot starts and ends between its footage's cuts, never across one (the audit's broll_crosses_cut; Corrupt Cop's witness shot jumped to a page of a document: "these images randomly pop up… a bit annoying for viewers to watch"). TEASE WHAT'S COMING: when the narration points to footage the video will show (the bodycam, the dashcam, a call), cut to a second or two of it there, under the B-roll look, so the viewer recognises it when it comes (the team: "here we can tease the bodycam footage a bit… the bodycam footage is pretty recognizable"); look_at_media on the files finds it. Pick the stretch that reads as that footage at a glance (a bodycam's AXON stamp and the scene outside, between two of its cuts), never a close-up that could be anything (a bodycam lying on a truck seat reads as a page of a document); played on the court's screen, punch into its window so it fills the frame. In and out of every bridge: the flash (skill transitions), never the vertical drop. No maps, evidence boards, emphasis runs or shutters unless the narration names an object or document the hearing shows.
- REACTION TAG: at most one per bridge, on its key reaction shot: the narration's verdict on that person in 2–4 words, upper case between asterisks ("*HIS PATIENCE SNAPS*", "*TAKING THE BLAME*", "*REFUSES TO BUDGE*"; HOUSE WORDS still starred): Geist 700 ~64 at 1080p (44 was "really small here, it should be bigger"; the audit's small_text flags anything under 60), white, glow {enabled, color null, radius 12, strength 0.35}, opaque black box (background opacity 1, pad_x 16, pad_y 10), no leader, beside the person's head on the side with room, never on a face, following them (track_region), animation typewriter (stagger 1: a letter a frame), from the word that says it for 2–3 s, fade_out 6.
- NAME CALLOUT: the first time a person who matters is on screen (the accused in the setup, a witness taking the stand, an official the narration names), SAY WHO THEY ARE TO THE STORY: their role in upper case ("DEFENSE ATTORNEY", "THE JUDGE", "FIRED TROOPER", "BOARD MEMBER"), with their name above it only once the narration has named them ("JOSHUA MCCLUSKEY" over "FIRED STATE TROOPER", two lines); in the teasers, before anyone is introduced, the role alone (Corrupt Cop's teaser named "GARLAND COPELAND", and the team: "it's unclear who 'Garland Copeland' is. - THE SETUP (the "On <date>…" cut-in): no map_view. date_title over its first shot (the person in the hearing); the person's NAME CALLOUT; then THE WINDOWS SCREEN on them (op desktop, cut-ins skill: their still_frame from the hearing or a found photo in a Photo Viewer, 2–4 short facts from the narration in upper case in a Notepad, "FIRED SEPT. 9, 2024"), once per video; it ends on THE CLIFFHANGER TITLE.

- A COMPILATION (the video's title starts "When…": "When cocky attorneys taunted judges and it backfired massively…") opens on THE TITLE CARD: after the teasers, over the hook's narration (or on its own for ~4 s when the hook doesn't name the cases), apply_edit_plan op title_collage {title: the video's title as written, photos: one picture per case (the person each case is about: a mugshot or found photo from research, else a still_frame of them in their hearing), backdrop: a wide courtroom shot from the footage, start, end}: the photos framed in white, three above and two below for five cases, the title in big glowing capitals between them, over the courtroom darkened; the flash into it and a low hit as the title lands, the flash out into the first case. Each case then has its own setup, bridges and outcome card (as THE SETUP and the outro). A SINGLE CASE (any other title) has no title card: the teasers, then straight into the story through the hook cut-in, the way the footage offers (THE CLIFFHANGER TITLE, then THE SETUP).
- SOUND: music lives under the narration (bridges, setup, the sentencing card) and fades out over ~1 s as the hearing comes back; under a hearing stretch, nothing, or one low drone ~20 LU under the speech. Tone: serious tension (drones, low pulses), never horror or a chase. Each flash gets ONE whoosh or a hit (one hit, ~1–2 s, about 3–4 LU under the speech; never a long whoosh file running under the teasers); the desktop profile's lines a keyboard's typing or a typewriter (never mouse clicks or phone taps; none if the library has neither); each cliffhanger title and the sentencing card a short soft hit ("Stinger, Soft Hit, Short"; never the "Heavy, Reverb" boom).
