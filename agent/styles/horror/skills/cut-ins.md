---
description: Cover every horror narration span with the story's own content, retro screenshots and slow, deliberate graphics.
---

Every narration span gets the picture its words name, chosen with the research ladder: the post or video itself, then the account, its bio, its comments and the news about it. A link in a line's notes (the script's comments: a study, an article, a post) is what that line shows, before any icon or text (the team: "I linked the study as a comment, so we should have shown it instead of the icon + text"). When a line is about an account ("his Instagram", what he posts, who he is), show the account itself: its profile, its posts, "we should scroll through this instagram profile", never b-roll in its place. A case the script gives no link for still gets its account: search_web its name ("Pacman Master instagram"), then capture_evidence the profile and posts; never a stretch of unrelated footage, or another case's, under it. Change the picture when the words move on, not on a timer: "once a shot or animation has made its point, move on". Each post the script links is laid out where it's linked, whole, with its sound: cut it to the seconds that show what its line said, a few seconds to ~15, never a whole 3-minute post; when the line only describes it (a bio, a pattern across posts) it can go silent under the narration instead. "The same clip never repeats in a video": show a post's different moments, then its profile, then what people said, never the same seconds twice (the audit's horror_repeated_clip).

**What Edit does by itself** (don't build these): cover_cut_in places the shots, tall reels fitted; a tall reel or phone video placed full frame stands over its own blurred, darkened copy ("for all the 9:16 videos it's better to have the full height… instead of fully zooming in": Edit undoes a zoom to fill); words are kept above pictures; every screenshot is its retro copy (softened, scan lines, a faint grid, torn edges on a card); a page fills the frame close on its words; the analog look goes over everything with commentary_look; a censor region is a mosaic. Place pictures plainly and let Edit dress them.

The guide's animations, each with its operation (apply_edit_plan unless it says tool):
- **Screenshots (the guide's look):** capture_evidence the whole page (an article, a thread, a channel, a bio), never a tight crop of one line: "Screenshots… should never look modern". commentary_card on it fills the frame with the page, close, the words read at the middle: `focus: [x, y]` in the image's pixels on the quoted words, slowly pushing in. capture_evidence takes a sign-in wall off the page (Instagram's profiles and posts come through whole: bio, counts, grid, caption, comments); full_page: true captures the whole page, and commentary_card {scroll: true} on it glides from its top to its bottom while the line runs. A post's caption quoted by the narration is highlighted on the post's own page ("Here we should have shown this with a marker effect").
- **Posts over footage (Animation 12):** "When showing a post from Reddit, Twitter, or any other forum, darken the b-roll playing in the background and add a drop shadow to the screenshot… apply a roughen edges effect": commentary_card with `full: false` on the post, footage still playing under it.
- **The highlighter (Animation 4):** "the one we use most": the tool highlight_evidence on the card's photo over the exact words the narration quotes (a bio line, a caption, a headline; never a photo in a profile's grid), one rectangle per line, red with roughened edges, drawn over about two-thirds of a second a line. "Sometimes we zoom in while doing the highlighter effect": the card's push (up to 1.15).
- **Pictures (Animation 5):** commentary_card with `photo: true` for any picture that isn't a page of words (a photo of a person or place, a still, a frame of footage): "a white outline with a glow effect and a drop shadow", sliding up slowly out of a blur. Without it, Edit treats the picture as a screenshot: the retro grid, full frame, waiting for a highlight. Moving footage stays footage, never a still of it.
- **3D (Animation 6):** "When showing images or news articles… try turning them into a 3D layer and adding a slight rotation": now and then the tool commentary_rotate on an article's or a channel's page.
- **Titles (Animations 2 and 7):** commentary_text rises out of a blur on the guide's curve (a fast start, a long settle). Its `look`: `bold` (Styks' and Nightmare Movies' big words, as written: "Bye /R9K/", "BANNED FROM BROADCAST"), `sans` (Sirius' dates: "April, 2006"), `serif` (a place), `caption` (words on the voice), `quote` (yellow, typed). Titles are white ("Text should be white, looks cleaner"). `at`: center, top, bottom (a date) or lower_left. A centred bold title blurs and darkens the picture behind it ("gaussian blur keyframes on the background"); `background_blur: false` for "the text slide-up without the blur". Words sit in one shot: they never stay up across a cut in the picture under them ("We switch the background while our text is still visible, makes it look unpolished"), and arrive with the narration, never while a post still plays its own sound ("we haven't switched to our voice over yet").
- **Chapters (Animation 7):** "Whenever we switch to a new topic or introduce a new chapter": commentary_chapter {text, sub} over the chapter's first footage, sub the small line above ("case 2:"). Dates slide up the same way: commentary_text {look: "sans", at: "bottom"}.
- **Places and names:** commentary_location {text: "Fort Myers, Florida"} (a soft serif in the middle); commentary_label {text: "Clifford Wallach"} (VHS capitals on a black box, lower left, typed) when a person is first seen.
- **Words on the voice (Animation 9):** "whenever we want to put more emphasis on certain words, for example, when describing a process": commentary_text {look: "caption", motion: "words", words_at: [each word's frame from the transcript]}, a few per case. Edit shows the line a short phrase at a time, each typed on alone and big ("the words should appear like this, one after another instead of all of them. It allows us to make each word bigger").
- **Typewriter (Animation 13):** "to emphasize a short phrase of 2–5 words or to quote someone": commentary_text {motion: "type"} (a quote: look quote), with a quiet keyboard sound.
- **A person and what they are (Animation 10):** "an image on the left or right with text on the opposite side, animated with a typewriter effect… perfect for quoting someone or adding short bullet points that describe a person": commentary_profile {media, side, quote} or {media, lines: [{text, at}]}. A quote beside a person's picture is always this, never a quote and a separate card on top of each other (the quote ended up behind the photo); when it replaces a card already there, that card goes.
- **An icon with its word (Animation 11):** commentary_icon {icon: person, phone, folder, document or camera; color; name}: "a red person over STALKER", "an old phone icon labeled 'Leaked messages'", "a Windows XP folder icon labeled 'Court documents'". Edit draws the icon.
- **The old desktop (Animation 1):** "only… occasionally, mainly for showing YouTube footage": the operation desktop with a video_player window playing it (Edit draws it as Internet Explorer on an old YouTube player, on an XP desktop), and a notepad window for a few lines typed, a photo_viewer for a picture (drawn as XP's Paint: "show his profile picture in another pop up… maybe just the old paint app"). For a video, a post, a channel or a person the story is about, not for screenshots. Never words alone for long: a Notepad gets the person's picture in a Paint window or the post beside it ("by now it's getting boring for viewers because they see the text for 20s+").
- **CC Lens:** "From time to time, we want to use a CC Lens effect": commentary_lens {start, end, strength 0.4–0.8} on a striking moment of footage, a few times a video.
- **A turn (Animation 8):** "we fade the first image or video down to 0% opacity, then fade the second one up from 0% to 100%, creating a slow fade through black": transition kind dip with frames 40–48, on a case or chapter change, not between every shot.

Animations "can take a bit longer than in other videos": a card holds while it's read (at least 3 s), a title arrives over most of a second. Nothing is shown "for longer than necessary". After composing each case, check_shots over it (two minutes at a time) and replace every shot whose picture doesn't show what its words name.
