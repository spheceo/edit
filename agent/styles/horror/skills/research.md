description: Find the real content behind every line: the posts themselves first, never a competitor's footage, stock only as a last resort.
---

"Always look for footage of the exact event, person, video, or place the voiceover is talking about. Every channel in our style references… uses real footage of the story for 75–90% of its B-roll. Stock footage is the exception, not the rule."

**For each voiceover line:**
1. Identify what the line is about: the person, the video or post, the place, the date, and the specific moment.
2. The original content first. If the line talks about a video, use that video; a livestream, a webcam, CCTV, a TV episode or a film, that footage. If the line describes a specific moment ("he turns the camera toward the window"), use the exact part where it happens.
3. If the original isn't available, other footage of the same event: news reports, police or bodycam footage, interviews with the people involved, footage of the real location.
4. Match the location literally: the real bridge, house, town, forest or building, ideally from around the time of the story.
5. If nothing from the same event exists, an indirect reference: a clip that "illustrates the point, as long as there's a real connection to the story" (the uploader's old channel page on the Wayback Machine for "quietly vanished from the internet"; a "This video has been removed" screen). Work out the point behind the line, find who or what in the story is a real example of it, and "only use the clip if you can explain why it fits in one sentence". Put that sentence in record_broll_observations.
6. Atmospheric footage only as a last resort, for abstract lines ("nobody knows what happened that night") or a bridge between sections. It still matches the line ("a forest story gets forest footage at night, not a random dark hallway") and the era (no modern phones, cars, LED lights or current UI in a 2006 story); "old camcorder, CCTV and home-video footage fits our analog look much better than clean 4K stock", handheld, low-light, a static CCTV angle; never cinematic, drone or slow-motion. Short, and never a whole section.

**Getting it.** import_broll takes a post's link as it is (an Instagram reel or carousel item with its img_index, an X, TikTok or Reddit video): the whole post comes in, or the range you give. YouTube ranges as usual, at most 240 s each. An image post, a profile, its bio or its comments: capture_evidence on the page (inspect it: a login wall is not evidence), or collect_images. "Many of our stories are about deleted or archived content": search YouTube for re-uploads (the original title, the uploader's username, "reupload", "deleted video", "archive", "mirror", "original"), the Wayback Machine for archived channel pages and sites (capture_evidence on its web.archive.org address), local news channels on YouTube ("[town] [event] news [year]"), and Twitter/X or Reddit only where the content was posted there.

**Never a competitor's footage.** "A competitor is any channel that makes iceberg, internet mystery, analog horror, lost media or 'disturbing internet' videos, and that includes every channel in our style references" (Styks, SIRIUS, Threadbare, nbody, Nightmare Movies, Abyssal Detective). "Our footage has to come from the original source: the uploader, the person involved, the news station or the archive, never from a channel that's already covering the story. If a re-upload clearly comes from a competitor (their logo, their edit, their text), keep looking for a cleaner copy. If you're unsure whether a channel is a competitor, don't use it."

**What makes a clip usable:** the subject (person, place or object) is clearly visible and as isolated as possible; no added text, re-uploader logos, watermarks, subtitles, reaction-cam frames or competitor edits; low quality is fine ("everything gets grain, vignette and chromatic aberration anyway"), but take the best version there is; it matches what the voiceover says ("don't show the wrong location, person or time period just because it looks similar"); "the same clip never repeats in a video".
