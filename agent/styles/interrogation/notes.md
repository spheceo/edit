INTERROGATION VIDEOS: a fixed camera on an interview room, the person questioned facing it, the detectives mostly with their backs to it. The video is that footage, mostly raw: stretches of one to three minutes as the Doc's ranges cut them, between short narration bridges (7–30 s) that say what the footage doesn't. The tape carries the raw stretches; the narration's opening and bridges are where the editing shows: there, what's said is SHOWN. The tape is usually 3:2 or 4:3 and wide: it always fills the frame (EVERY PICTURE FILLS THE FRAME), raw or not.
- SEVERAL CASES: a script can be a compilation (several cases, one Doc, or a Doc whose notes bring in each case's own script). Each case has its own footage, research, opening teasers and hook, date cut-in (its first narration after its teasers, or after "Moving on to the next case…") and outcome narration, which is that case's outro wherever it sits. Work stage by stage, every case in a stage before the next stage (get_edit_context with start/end per case). A narration whose note says "Reuse: <link>" is followed by a finished video of ours, imported whole: nothing over it, no flash out of it, set captioned (set_segments) before subtitling.
- "Note to editor: Blur this" on a narration word ("murder", "killed") means the word is censored, never a blur on the picture: starred wherever it's on screen ("m****r") and muted in the voice-over (final pass). Murder, blood and strip are starred everywhere even unmarked.
- THE OPENING (the teasers, their hooks and each case's backstory, up to the frame the tape rolls) is built the way Red Thread opens: graphics on screen most of the way (60–75%, never more than ~5 s without one), then none once the tape rolls (intro skill).
- THE RAW TAPE (the Doc's ranges after the opening) plays clean, as Red Thread's and Dr Insanity's do: filled, no punch-ins or effects, no music. Clean of the uploader too: a tape downloaded from another channel often has their subtitles burned in along the bottom, their name or a faint watermark across the room, a credit, or their own small box of other footage; ours goes on top of none of it. Its one fixed framing (the same scale and y for the whole tape, set once when it's placed, not a punch-in) puts their subtitles below the frame (audit burned_subtitles names the scale and y) and a corner credit or box past the edge; a mark in the middle gets a rect blur for the tape's length (watermarked_footage). Every line said in it gets bodycam's subtitles, in the speaker's colour (subtitles skill). Every narration is entered and left with THE BRIDGE FLASH; everything else is a hard cut (transitions skill). The narration is never "clean": see THE BRIDGES.
- THE BRIDGES (every narration after the opening) SHOW WHAT'S SAID, as the references do under their narrators (the team: "We say a lot of things right here in the voiceover that we could literally show"; "I don't like that we just play the same footage here for so long. I need to see some more going on on the screen, at least some zooms and some different effects"). Read the bridge sentence by sentence: each person, place, thing, time or event it names gets its picture as it's said: the person's photo in a File Viewer with their name (op file_viewer name) or a photo inset beside the person questioned; the place on the map; the article, record or document; their own words as a red quote; the turn of the story as a text slam; evidence on the evidence board; a span of years on the timeline bar. A new picture every ~2–4 s, varied across the video (the Windows screen at most one bridge in three; never the same picture twice). Pictures, not just words: a text slam is for the story's turn, not a stand-in for every picture; when the research has no picture of what a bridge names, find one (collect_images, use_found) before falling back to the room (audit bridge_unillustrated). The ROOM under a narration is for lines about the person questioned or what's happening in the room, and it's never played plain: it's TREATED, as Red Thread's bridges are: black and white with the edges darkened and soft (an adjustment {grade {saturation -1, vignette ~0.6}} with op film_look {soft_edges: true, strength "light"}), closer than the raw tape (the team: "we need to be more zoomed in here"): a push you see move (cover_cut_in push [1, 1.25] at speed 1, not a barely-there 1.08) and, every other shot or so, a punch-in on the person questioned's face (scale 1.5–2 on their face), a freeze on them with a hit (audit room_wide). Nothing plain on screen more than ~4 s: cover the bridge's room with a shot every ~3–4 s (cover_cut_in with many shots: other moments, punch-ins on a face), and a picture over part of a shot doesn't make the rest of it new (audit long_broll_shot counts only what shows). A File Viewer or inset sits on the far side from the person questioned, never over their face (audit picture_on_face). Room shots never show a moment before it has played (a confession, a breakdown, a photo being shown).
- THE NAME CALLOUT is how a person questioned is introduced, once, when the narration first names them over their footage (the prologue's room), never as big faint letters across the room (the team: "we need to add her name next to her in a different way… a black box identification where we have a line and then a box pops up next to her face"): full name and age ("Steven Mark Zelich, 54") small beside their head: white Geist 500 ~26, glow off, background {color [0,0,0,1], opacity 0.8, pad_x 10, pad_y 6}, typed on (typewriter, stagger 1), a thin white leader from the neck (leader {color [1,1,1,0.9], width 2, attach "side"}, track_region target "leader" on their face; the room's camera doesn't move, so when track_region can't follow a small face, give the leader's x/y at their neck yourself rather than dropping the line), held ~3 s, fade_out 6. A detective the narration names gets the same with their role ("Det. Nicona Cordero").
- THE CLOSING LINE ON SCREEN (two or three a video, the strongest hooks): a bridge's last sentence typed on as it's said, over the picture blurred and darkened (an adjustment {grade {exposure -1.2}, blur {radius ~40}} beneath it), white Geist 700 ~62, centred, max_width 0.8*width, glow, typewriter in step with the voice, held to the bridge's end.
- MUSIC: under the opening, the bridges and the outros only, ONE BED PER SECTION (the cold open, the prologue, each bridge, each outro), kept the whole way through it: the music changes only where what we hear changes (the team: "we only need to change the music if there is some switch in what we hear. Otherwise, it doesn't make sense to change the music"; audit music_change_mid_narration). It comes in with a bridge's flash and fades out (~1 s) across the flash back into the room. The beds come from the library's Investigative (evidence, timelines, background), Low tones (regular narration), Building tension (before a turn) and Revelation (the reveal) categories; the same bed may carry several bridges. Never the Dark ambient drones (the guide's "murder cases or disturbing scenes, for example bodycam footage at the scene") or slow, sad music under a bridge that lays out facts or suspicion ("too slow and too almost emotional… this sounds like funeral music"; audit music_mood), and the Pulsing and High-pace beds only for an intro. Low under the narration: ~16 LU under the voice (audit music_loud). The raw interrogation has no music: the room's sound carries it.
