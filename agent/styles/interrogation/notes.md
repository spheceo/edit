INTERROGATION VIDEOS: a fixed camera on an interview room, the person questioned facing it. The video is that footage, mostly raw (stretches of one to three minutes, as the Doc's ranges cut them), between short narration bridges (7–30 s) that say what the footage doesn't. The tape is usually 3:2 or 4:3: it always fills the frame (EVERY PICTURE FILLS THE FRAME).
- SEVERAL CASES: a compilation has, per case, its own footage, research, opening (teasers, hook, date cut-in) and outcome narration (its outro, wherever it sits). Work stage by stage, every case in a stage before the next (get_edit_context with start/end per case). At "in a similar case…" the next case starts straight away. A narration whose note says "Reuse: <link>" is followed by a finished video of ours, imported whole: nothing over it, set captioned (set_segments) before subtitling.
- "Note to editor: Blur this" on a narration word ("murder") means the word is starred wherever it's on screen ("m****r") and muted in the voice-over, never a blur on the picture. Murder, blood and strip are starred everywhere even unmarked.
- THE OPENING (teasers, hooks, each case's backstory, up to the frame the tape rolls): intro skill.
- THE RAW TAPE (the ranges after the opening) plays clean: filled, one fixed framing for the whole tape (set once when placed, not a punch-in), no effects, no music, the room's own sound; subtitles on every line. Clean of the uploader too: their burned-in subtitles go below the frame (audit burned_subtitles gives the scale and y), a corner credit past the edge, a mark in the middle under a rect blur (watermarked_footage). Every narration is entered and left with THE BRIDGE FLASH (transitions skill).
- THE BRIDGES (every narration after the opening) SHOW WHAT'S SAID (the team: "We say a lot of things right here in the voiceover that we could literally show"): sentence by sentence, each person, place, record, quote or event gets its picture as it's named: a person's photo in a File Viewer with their name; the place on the map; the document; their own words as a red quote; a span of years on the timeline bar; a list of facts on the Windows screen (at most one bridge in three). A new picture every ~2–4 s, and every picture makes sense with the words under it ("We simply need to illustrate things that make it really, really easy for the viewer to understand"; audit bridge_unillustrated: find a picture with collect_images before falling back to the room).
  - ONE FACE PER PERSON: each person keeps the one photo (two at most) they were introduced with, every time they come up, so the viewer learns them ("I think we should stick to one image… that'll make it a lot easier for the viewer"). Variety comes from places, records and quotes, never from new photos of the same person.
  - THE ROOM under a narration (only for lines about the person questioned or what's happening in the room) is never plain: treated, closer than the tape (a push you see move, cover_cut_in push [1, 1.25]; a punch-in on their face every other shot or so), a new shot every ~3–4 s (audit long_broll_shot, room_wide). The treatment changes from bridge to bridge (the team: "we don't just have the exact same overlay for every single cutting"): black and white with soft dark edges (an adjustment {grade {saturation -1, vignette ~0.6}} with op film_look {soft_edges: true, strength "light"}), a cold desaturated grade, a darker high-contrast look; never so blurred you can't see the person ("We can barely see her because it's blurry"). A bridge's look holds to its flash out: never back to colour inside it. No zoom-outs, no back-and-forth moves. A File Viewer or inset sits on the far side from the person's face (audit picture_on_face). Room shots never show a moment before it has played.
- THE NAME of the person questioned, once, where the narration first names them over their footage: their photo in a File Viewer beside them (never over their face), the name and age right under the picture (op file_viewer name: "AVA RAE MOORING, 18"), a subtle click as it opens (the team: "just have the text stand right under the image and then just show the image in a file viewer, where we have a subtle click when it appears"). With no photo, a small black name box beside their head with a thin white line from their shoulder (leader {color [1,1,1,0.9], width 2, attach "side"} at their shoulder; never floating, never across the room). A detective the narration names gets the box with their role ("Det. Kevin Brewer").
- THE PAUSE, instead of words in the middle of the screen (the team: "We can't really have this text in the middle of the screen. What we could maybe do instead is pause the previous frame and then write June 2013 on that frame, and then zoom over to the Notepad"): to say something over the interrogation (a date, the story's turn, a bridge's strongest line), freeze the room on the frame before (still_frame), push in slowly, and write it on that frame, small and set beside the person, or open a Notepad over it. Big centred text is for one or two turns a video at most. A Notepad's lines are short, in the order things happened, and read on their own ("if you don't listen and just look at the notepad, it still makes sense").
- MUSIC only under the opening, the bridges and the outros, ONE BED PER SECTION (the cold open, the prologue, each bridge, each outro), changing only where what we hear changes ("we only need to change the music if there is some switch in what we hear"; audit music_change_mid_narration). A bed comes in with a bridge's flash and fades (~1 s) across the flash back into the room. Investigative, Low tones, Building tension and Revelation beds; never Dark ambient drones or slow, sad music under facts or suspicion ("this sounds like funeral music"; audit music_mood); Pulsing and High-pace only in an intro. A song plays only its good stretch: where a bed runs long, its best first seconds repeat rather than the song playing on ("It's only the first 5 or 6 seconds that work"). Quiet: ~20 LU under the voice (audit music_loud; "We need to turn down the music significantly"). The raw interrogation has none.
