- THE OPENING (interrogation), built the way Red Thread Interrogations opens: teaser lines from the interrogation (the Doc's first ranges) with short narration hooks between them, then THE PROLOGUE (the case's backstory) up to the frame the tape rolls. In a compilation every case opens this way. Each teaser ends on a finished sentence. The first 30 seconds decide whether anyone watches: every cut clean, every picture clear, nothing odd.
- ONE LOOK over the whole opening: a dark vignette (an adjustment {grade {vignette 0.7, contrast 0.1}}) across the teasers, hooks and prologue alike, on from the first frame and never fading in or out mid-shot (the team: "either vignette everywhere or no vignette everywhere… vignette everywhere, so please fix this throughout the entire intro. Shouldn't be inside of the video, though"): none on the raw tape. No colour flashes or tints anywhere ("the screen doesn't turn into colors").
- THE COLD OPEN (the teasers and their hooks):
  - It opens on the people: the first frame shows the person questioned, punched in on them, never an empty corner of the room ("We need to be able to see the occupants"). When the first range starts before they're in shot (an empty room, the door), its picture starts later in the tape, where they are, while its sound stays where it was.
  - The teasers are clear: in their own colour and sharp under the one vignette, never black and white or softened like a bridge's room ("We can barely see her because it's blurry").
  - Every teaser is PUNCHED IN CLOSE on the person questioned: a closer framing (scale), not a zoom move: head and shoulders filling a good part of the frame's height, often 1.8–2.5× the filled frame, x/y on their face, kept inside the picture, a new framing each teaser and held (audit teaser_wide: none left wide; "It doesn't make any sense to not be zoomed into anything right here"). What the team dislikes is a zoom MOVE on every clip: a slow push only on the line that lands, a few in the cold open, never a zoom out ("the zooms don't come in with every single clip… Red Thread… do it in a far cleaner way").
  - TIGHT: no dead air; a pause between a teaser's lines is cut out (remove_time; the punch-in changes on the cut so it reads as a jump cut; audit dead_air).
  - THE TEASERS' CAPTIONS: once the subtitles are on, caption_speakers over the teasers (start 0, end: the prologue's first frame, seconds): each line as its speaker's caption, word by word as it's said, in their colour, glowing, as every bodycam intro has them (the user, Oct 29, comparing two edits: "the intro of the off-duty officer uses the correct subtitle style but for the predator card it does not"; audit intro_uncaptioned). Run it again after any later add_subtitles over the intro.
  - EACH TEASER ENDS ON ITS CUE: the Doc's comment on a range ("found...girl") names its first and last words; the teaser plays from the first to the last, never stopping a word or a sentence short ("we should … see where the end word is supposed to be, and cut according to that"). realign_script with start/end over the intro puts any that doesn't back on its words.
  - The hooks' pictures, changing every few seconds on what the narration names, each FULL FRAME and clear to someone who's never heard of the case: a person as the narration names them, in a File Viewer with their name (their one photo for the whole video); the person questioned's photo under the film look ("[ACTUAL PHOTO]"); a red quote of the line that gives them away (op red_quote); the facts the hook lists in a Notepad on the Windows screen, once. Never a picture nobody can place. At most one text_slam in the cold open.
  - Into the first hook the sound says the story is starting: a riser building into the cut, a hit on it, the music changing there (audit hook_unmarked).
  - Cuts: hard cuts between teasers and screens; a short dip to black on the hardest beats. A click on every picture that pops up.
  - THE COLD OPEN ENDS on a dip to black with ~0.5 s of silence, and a sound that tells the viewer the video is starting: a riser into the black and a deep hit on the first frame after ("We need to have a transition, maybe a dip to black… half a second of silence before we start"; "some kind of sound effect here prior to us starting the actual video"). Nothing said or subtitled under the black (audit line_under_black).
- THE PROLOGUE (the backstory and date cut-in, up to the first interrogation range): op countdown {start, end: the frame the tape rolls} over all of it, and:
  - THE MAP: map_view of the place, top-down or a gentle angle, tone "dark", marker "building" or "dot"; date_title {position "bottom", place "City, State", text: the date}; op place_label typing the place's name; the people the narration names over it in File Viewers with their names.
  - THE STORY behind the case on the Windows screen when it's a list of facts or several people; otherwise film-look photos.
  - THE ROOM introduced, treated as a bridge's room (style notes), with THE NAME of the person questioned as the narration names them (style notes) and the people the narration ties them to beside them as they're named (their File Viewers). Never plain more than ~4 s.
  - fast_forward over the room at 4–8× (muted) when the narration skips hours; timeline_bar when it spans years.
  - THE TAPE ROLLS when the countdown reaches 00:01 on the prologue's last picture, and the viewer feels it ("we need to be able, as viewers, to know and feel that, 'Okay, now the video starts.'"): a riser over the countdown's last ~2 s, a deep hit on the tape's first frame, the prologue's music cut dead there, then only the room's sound (audit tape_start_unmarked). No disclaimer unless the user asks for one (op disclaimer over the last ~5 s before the tape).
