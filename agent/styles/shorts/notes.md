A SHORT is one video's highlights, 45–60 seconds, in a 9:16 frame (1080×1920): bodycam, a dashcam, an interrogation, a news clip. The footage tells it with its own sound: no narration, no voice-over, no cut-ins, teasers, intro captions, maps, Windows desktop, outro or sentencing card, credits or end screen. The house rules for those don't apply here; subtitles, house words, blurring and the people's labels and colours do.

What the reference does (InOutNetwork, measured): 30–47 seconds; a hard cut every 2–8 seconds, never a transition; the first frame is already the story (a line mid-conversation, the moment things turn), no title card; it ends on the payoff (the arrest, the punchline, the reveal) and stops, no wind-down. Every shot fills the 9:16 frame, the 16:9 picture cropped to its height and moved so the person who matters is in the middle ("making keyframes so we move around on the screen and always center what has to be centered"). Subtitles a word or three at a time, a little below the middle, each under its speaker's coloured label (Officer:, Suspect:, Victim:) and cut straight from one to the next. When something important happens without words (a chase, a fall), a line says it under that person's label ("Running Away", "Chasing", "Falls Down"). The person the story is about is introduced once, as they first appear, with a callout: the picture freezes on them, cut in and brightened, "WANTED SUSPECT" above them in heavy white capitals and a white hand-drawn arrow to them, about a second (shorts_callout). Bodycam stamps and the uploader's marks are blurred, swears starred and muted.

Music: one of the team's Shorts songs under the whole Short, low under the voices (music-and-sound).
