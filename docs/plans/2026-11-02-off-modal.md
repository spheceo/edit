# Edit off Modal: everything on the team's Macs, still cloud-shared

Sphe, Nov 2: Edit moves off Modal. Imports, playback and exports run on the team's Apple Silicon Macs (Sphe's M1, 8 GB, 15 GB free; JJ's M4 Pro, 24 GB), and everything stays shared through the cloud. The audit and its measurements are in the T3 thread "Apple Silicon Edit Speed vs Modal". Each step ships and is committed on its own, measured before and after.

## 1. Cache budget sized to the disk

Before: one budget of 400 MB for everything Edit keeps (storage.ts), made for a Mac that left its heavy work to Modal. This M1 on Nov 2: 15 GB free, a 6.7 GB cache (3.2 GB of it an export's chunks, 0.59 GB of cloud copies). Every hour the cleaner was 6.3 GB over, so every cloud copy older than six hours went (0.62 GB here), whoever's it was, and playing a project again read its previews from R2 again: the log's hourly "freed" lines run 0.01–0.67 GB on Oct 31 and Nov 1.

After: `localBudgetBytes` is a quarter of the room (free space plus the cache), between 400 MB and 50 GB, and always leaves 1 GB free: 5.4 GB on this M1. `EDIT_CACHE_BUDGET_MB` still overrides. Past it, copies of R2 objects go by project: footage no project here uses, then a done (Home's Done) or archived project's, then the projects this Mac opened longest ago (`opened.ts`, written when the editor opens a project and every minute it stays open). A project open now or in the last twelve hours keeps all of its; with the disk under 1 GB free, only one open in the last ten minutes does. Block folders now record their key, so a playback render's blocks are known to be its project's.

## 3. Export slots by encode engines

Before: one export per 12 GB of memory (export_capacity.ts), so JJ's 24 GB M4 Pro ran two at once, and they shared its one video encode engine: that's the encoder stall of Nov 2. Measured since: an Apple chip has one encode engine (M1–M4 and the Pros), two on a Max, four on an Ultra. After: one export per engine from the chip's name (`machdep.cpu.brand_string`: "Apple M1" → 1 here; EDIT_LOCAL_EXPORTS still overrides), still gated by memory and load. A second export waits and says why: "Queued · waiting for the encoder (this Mac has one)" (`waiting` on the export's activity, shown in the Exports panel).

## 2. Exports read local excerpts

Before: an export of a cloud edit read every original through a presigned URL, one FFmpeg process per clip, each restart paying R2's ~0.25–0.5 s from South Africa. Kroger (148,463 frames, 414 clips, 63 originals used, 2.1 GB of them) took 65 min 11 s on this M1 on Nov 1 (38 fps).

After: before rendering, every 30 s cell of an original the timeline shows is cut to the cache, picture stream-copied, starting on the original's frame grid (`export_excerpts.ts`); the engine's reader of that URL reads the local excerpt where one holds the time (`crates/edit-engine/src/excerpts.rs`, `VideoReader::read_excerpt`), with AVFoundation. FFmpeg's stream copy writes an edit list so the excerpt's time 0 is the cell's start exactly; AVFoundation and FFmpeg both honour it (render.rs tests: frame for frame with B-frames and a cut off a keyframe, and with a GOP longer than a cell at 29.97 fps). Chunk keys don't change (the scene and the URL's object are the same), so re-exports still copy their unchanged chunks. Kroger's 239 cells of 91 originals (1.6 GB) were cut in 45–69 s cold, found in 8.5 s warm.

Measured: the render of Kroger itself is not faster. Clean (no swapping, 23:02–23:16) it ran at ~32 fps; Modal's run of it was ~21 fps a GPU section, so Kroger is a heavy timeline, and its time goes to compositing and to the native reader starting a new AVAssetReader on every jump (`mac_media.m restart`), not mostly to the network. On the fixed build (Nov 3, 00:29–01:46, under load 16–44 from other threads' builds and servers, so not a clean number) Kroger exported whole in 77 min 00 s (32.1 fps) through one memory failure on this 8 GB Mac in swap and one false stall restart (fixed, 95837147); its last attempt composited 104,992 frames in 42 min 03 s and waited 1 min 40 s on decoding and 32 min 03 s on encoding: with the excerpts, decoding no longer limits it; the encoder's side does (readback, swizzle, VideoToolbox: step 7). Every one of its 368 cuts shows the same frames as Nov 1's export over URLs (a first pass flagged 5; each checked frame by frame, -2 to +2, identical). Chunk keys don't change with excerpts: two runs on one build reused each other's 21 chunks; a new build changes every key by design (the code's hash is in it).

Found and fixed on the way (each on Kroger, each tested): the addon written in place under a running export by `just dev`'s watch (8c3faf1f); the compositor's error hidden behind "the export stopped part-way through a chunk" (8c3faf1f); stills sent to the engine as URLs, which ImageIO can't read ("ImageIO couldn't decode the image", 17 min in: they're fetched to the cache first); a frame asked a hair past an original's last cell read from a URL reader with nothing decoded ("could not decode <url>: failed to fill whole buffer": the last cell reaches on past the end); the stall watch taking an engine retry's lower progress for no progress (95837147).

Open: excerpts in R2, shared between Macs, once uplink isn't the bottleneck (Kroger's took longer to upload than to cut); an A/B of the same spans over URLs and from excerpts on a quiet Mac; the reader's AVAssetReader per jump (a reader that seeks instead).

## 4. Smooth playback on the Mac

Before: a cloud edit's smooth playback rendered on Modal; with Modal off, here, as one file the viewer could play only once it was whole. Sphe watched "Smooth playback 59%" on the 26-minute 16-Year-Old (48,112 frames): the render took 16 min 49 s here (48 fps), and the first 2 minutes of it were the sound's mix before any picture.

After: (a) a render of the same picture already in R2 (Modal's) is played at once; (c) one Modal can't make is made here; (d) the render after an agent's turn runs here, behind exports; (e) kept at the project's size and "small" (the native chunked writer takes no other size; a scaled output comes with step 7); (f) the render does the chunks from the viewer's playhead first (`Plan.focus`: the minute from it, then the sound's mix, then the rest), writes its plan as it starts, and the viewer plays each chunk as it lands (`playback_stream.ts`, `lib/smoothStream.ts`: a MediaSource of fragmented-MP4 chunks around the playhead, the sound in an `<audio>` kept in step). Modal is off by default (`EDIT_MODAL_EXPORTS`, the Justfile and the trial server too). (b), a Mac's own render in R2, is held: whole it's ~900 MB up from South Africa after every turn; chunk by chunk later.

Measured on the 16-Year-Old: a finished render shows at a seek in 0.5–1.6 s and plays at 30 fps (146–151 frames in 5 s); a render under way showed its first chunk 17 s after it started (142 s before, the whole mix first); on a Mac with nothing cached, under load (another thread's builds and server), 138 s, the proxies' blocks read from R2 for the first time. Found on the way: a chunk out of timeline order took the next frame into its encoder ("Native encoder: The operation could not be completed"), a half-written mix served as the sound, a mix from another process taken for this render's, and a page that never asked again after a server restart; all fixed and tested.

## 9. Export retention

Rule (Sphe, Nov 2): the newest export of each project stays (re-exports copy from it); an older one goes once a newer export of the project is a week old, shared or not, its entry kept in exports.json and marked removed with where it still is (the review link's version, or Drive); with the disk under the 1 GB floor, older ones go at once, the oldest first, until there's room. Never the newest, never one exporting, being read, sharing or uploading, only files in Edit's export folders and on the list. Runs with the cache's cleaner; `bun scripts/export-retention.ts [home] [--delete]` reports (or deletes) from a data folder's files.

Measured on this Mac: the report on ~/.edit (32 projects) found nothing to delete, and the deleting run deleted nothing and left exports.json as it was: every export this Mac made has been archived to the cloud (~/Movies/Edit holds 7.8 MB), so Kroger's v4–v6 are on another Mac. The dev and trial homes: nothing either.

## The two bugs left from the first run

**A restart while an export is archiving re-ran the whole export.** The export's job (`jobs.json`) ended only after its file was uploaded to R2, so a restart during the upload started the export over (Kroger: 77 minutes, from nothing). Now the export's job ends when its file is whole and listed, and the upload is a job of its own (`archive`), which a restart sends again (`archiveFinished`), never rendering. Tested: while the upload runs, the list holds the archive and not the export; resuming it uploads and lists, nothing renders.

**Playback renders read proxies through FFmpeg over this server's preview URL**, one FFmpeg process a clip (the 16-Year-Old rendered at 48 fps). Now the render cuts the proxies' stretches it plays into local cells first, as an export's originals are (`export_excerpts.prepare`, keys `edit/excerpts/proxies/…`), pinned while it renders, and the engine reads them with AVFoundation. On the way: the engine named every proxy read through the preview path as one object (`http://127.0.0.1:7700/api/cloud/preview`, the query dropped), in its chunk keys and its excerpts alike, so chunks of different proxies with the same times could be taken for each other's; an object is now `cloud:<key>` for such a URL, whatever the port (`util::url_object`). Measured on the 16-Year-Old (48,112 frames): **6 min 22 s (126 fps)**, against 16 min 49 s (48 fps); its first minute from the playhead in 21.6 s, then the sound's mix (1 min 11 s), then the rest (4 min 45 s, waiting 57 s on decoding). Its 72 cells of 10 proxies were here already (cut by an earlier render); from cold they'd add about 15 s (Kroger's 239 took 42 s).

## 8. YouTube preset

`ExportOptions.preset` (export.rs `Preset`: `house`, the default and the house export as it was, or `youtube`). YouTube's: H.264 High, 4:2:0, BT.709 (as every finished export already is), a keyframe every half second (`key_interval`: 15 frames at 30 fps, the native writer and FFmpeg's `-g` alike, against two seconds for the house export), AAC-LC 48 kHz stereo at 384 kbps (the house export's is 256), the High bitrate tier whatever quality is asked (~8.7 Mbit/s at 1080p30, the house export's own), and −14 LUFS unless another loudness is asked, through the mixer's look-ahead limiter that holds peaks under −1 dBTP. The Share dialog has "For YouTube" beside Watermark; the choice is kept for the project's later shares like its other settings. Tried on a 30 s Kroger span: H.264 High, yuv420p, BT.709 throughout, 1080p30 at 8.63 Mbit/s, keyframes at 0, 0.5, 0.93, 1.37 s (never more than half a second apart), AAC-LC 48 kHz stereo, −14.2 LUFS with the peak at −2.0 dBFS (the log: "−22.2 LUFS, true peak −2.0 dBTP: +8.9 dB to −14.0 LUFS"). One thing differs from the ask: the sound is 320 kbps, not 384. Apple's AAC encoder refuses anything above 320 kbps at 48 kHz stereo ("Cannot Encode Media", which failed the first try), and FFmpeg's own, asked for 384, makes ~250 kbps even of pink noise; 320 through Apple's is the most there is here (308 kbps measured).

## 5. Playback reads: prefetch, the scrub tier, the edge cache

**Prefetch.** When a project opens, and again from the playhead whenever the viewer asks for smooth playback, the proxies' stretches the timeline plays are read into the block cache in timeline order (`cloud_media.prefetchProject`, `cloud_blocks.prefetch`): ten minutes of the timeline or 600 MB at most, two blocks at a time and only while the viewer's own reads leave two of the six R2 reads free, so it never holds the viewer up. The cache's budget (step 1) evicts it like any block.

**The scrub tier** (`scrub.ts`, the page's `lib/playback.ts`). Beside each video's 1080p proxy, a 960×540 copy with every frame a keyframe (all-intra H.264 at ~6 Mbit/s on VideoToolbox), made only for the stretches the timeline uses, in pieces of five minutes of the source, on demand: as a drag starts, the page asks for the pieces from a minute before the playhead to two after and polls which are made. While the playhead is dragged the viewer decodes the piece's own frame (one decode a position); resting or playing, the 1080p proxy, so what's judged is full size. Pieces are kept in this Mac's cache (`cloud/scrub/…`, evicted like any copy), not in R2 (Nov 3: a piece is made here in ~13 s, and fetching its 225 MB from South Africa takes longer and competes with footage uploads); with `EDIT_EDGE_MEDIA=1` they also go to R2 at `edit/scrub/<proxy>/<n>.mp4` and another Mac fetches them. One is made at a time, never beside an export. Where a piece isn't made yet, the proxy is the only tier. Measured on the 16-Year-Old (dev, headless Chrome, a drag of 300 positions a frame apart every 33 ms across one 24 fps clip: 240 of its frames asked): the proxy alone showed 21–22 of them (9%; docs/preview.md's "about half" was a lighter edit); with the scrub tier, 226 and then 240 (94%, 100%), 221 of the drag's pictures the tier's at 960×540, and the full 1080p proxy's exact frame once it rested. A five-minute piece takes 13 s to make on this M1 and is 225 MB (7,200 frames, every one a keyframe). Found on the way: a rough request skipped any picture within half a second (right for the proxy's keyframes, wrong for a tier of all keyframes); each new position stopped the decode before it; and the draw's stand-ins looked only at the proxy's pictures, so the tier's frames landed after the playhead had moved on. All three fixed (the tier's reach is half a frame, one decode a lane with the newest position waiting, and its nearest frame within a quarter second stands in).

**The edge cache, code side** (`apps/relay/src/media.ts`, behind `EDIT_EDGE_MEDIA=1` on the Mac). R2's presigned URLs point at `<account>.r2.cloudflarestorage.com`, which Cloudflare's edge never caches: every Mac in South Africa pays R2's ~0.25 s for every 2 MiB block, even right after a teammate read it. With the setting on, the Mac asks the relay for a proxy's address with `edge: true`, and a relay that has `MEDIA_HOST` and `MEDIA_SECRET` hands out `https://<MEDIA_HOST>/v1/media/<key>?exp=&sig=` instead: signed by the relay for that key and time (the URL is the grant: no sign-in on each read, as a presigned URL), only for proxies (`edit/proxies/…`, which never change), each 2 MiB range the Mac asks for kept in the edge's cache for a year under the object and range, whoever's signature asked. Without both settings (or the setting on the Mac), nothing changes. Tests: the address only for proxies, a range from R2 once and from the edge after for another Mac's address, a forged or lapsed signature refused.

**What only Sphe can do (the Cloudflare dashboard).** The Cache API does nothing on `*.workers.dev`, so the relay needs a custom domain on the team's zone:

1. Cloudflare dashboard → Workers & Pages → `edit-relay` → Settings → Domains & Routes → Add → Custom domain → `media.spheceo.com` (Cloudflare makes the DNS record). The same for `edit-relay-dev` → `media-dev.spheceo.com`.
2. No cache rule is needed: the Worker keeps the ranges itself (`caches.default`), which works on a custom domain. Leave Caching → Cache Rules as it is.
3. Infisical (Platform project, folder `/edit-relay`): add `MEDIA_SECRET` (a long random string, `openssl rand -hex 32`) in dev and in prod, then `bun scripts/push-secrets.ts --yes` (dev) and `bun scripts/push-secrets.ts --env prod --yes` (the live relay) in `app/apps/relay`.
4. Tell me the domains: I add `MEDIA_HOST = "media.spheceo.com"` to `[vars]` and `MEDIA_HOST = "media-dev.spheceo.com"` to `[env.dev.vars]` in `wrangler.toml`; dev deploys by hand, prod with the next nightly.
5. Then `EDIT_EDGE_MEDIA=1` on the Macs (made the default once a teammate's second read of a block is measured from Johannesburg's cache).

## 7. Zero-copy: measured, decided against; the blur is the cost

**The first split (Nov 3, night) undercounted the readback.** Its null sink (`EDIT_EXPORT_SINK=null`) still copies each frame out of the GPU and maps it, which is what zero-copy removes, so its 38 s "waiting on encoding" was the render and the readback together. `EDIT_EXPORT_SINK=none` now submits each frame's render and waits on the device only (no `copy_output_to`, no map), and `EDIT_EXPORT_DROP` leaves effects out of every frame (`blur`, `adjust`, `text`, `waveform`, `solid`, `overlays`: all but the bottom video), logging the layers a frame the span draws.

**Measured on the same five minutes of Kroger (frames 30,000–39,000, excerpts cut already), each run after 90 s of rest.** This MacBook Air has no fan and throttles under a sustained GPU load: back-to-back runs drifted from 40 s to 1 min 29 s for the same render, so only rested runs are compared. Its layers a frame: video 1.00, text 0.25, adjustments 0.35 (0.28 of them blurring), solids 0.07.

| run | compositing | waited on the GPU/encoder |
|---|---|---|
| encoded (as an export) | 1 min 00 s | 42.7 s |
| null sink (rendered, read back, not encoded) | 41.0 s | 20.8 s |
| render only (`none`: no readback) | 40.4 s | 21.1 s |
| render only, no blur | 21.6 s | 0.4 s |
| render only, no text | 39.2 s | 21.0 s |
| render only, no adjustment layers | 21.3 s | 0.2 s |
| render only, the bottom video alone | 21.2 s | 0.1 s |

**Decision: zero-copy stays unbuilt.** The readback is ~0.6 s of the 60 (render only 40.4 s against 41.0 s with it). The encoder's side (VideoToolbox, the swizzle, the copy into its buffer) is the next 19 s, and zero-copy would take only the copies out of that. The render is the remaining 40 s, and **the blur is half of it**: with blur left out the render takes 21.6 s, the same as the bottom video alone (21.2 s), and the GPU wait goes from 21 s to nothing, the decoders becoming the limit (5.4 s). Text costs ~1 s, grades and the rest of the adjustments nothing measurable.

Why the blur costs so much (edit-render `blur_region`): both passes of every blurring adjustment run over the whole 1920×1080 frame with up to 48 taps each side (97 texture reads a pixel a pass), even when the region is one face. The second pass only masks the region in the shader. **The next pass is the blur, not zero-copy:** draw both passes only over the region's box grown by 3σ and the feather (a face is a few percent of the frame), and for a large σ blur at half or quarter size and scale up (a Gaussian's result at σ 35 px is smooth at a quarter of the pixels). On a span like this one that takes the render from 40 s toward 21 s, about a third of the export's 60 s, against zero-copy's one second.

## 6. Imports without whole-file proxies: designed, not built

The proxy is already the media's preview copy (one file, linked). What's left changes what every reader of a cloud media assumes (one proxy a media: playback's blocks, transcription, the watcher, frame grabs, the agent's looks, playback renders): proxy pieces of ~5 minutes by file and range, made on demand when the timeline, the viewer or the agent touches a stretch, would need each of those to ask for a stretch and its piece; sound and transcript first (transcription reads the sound copy, not the proxy) and one decode a file for thumbnails, peaks, loudness, censor greys and the proxy are each their own change in media.ts and the engine's media.rs. The scrub tier's pieces (`scrub.ts`) are the pattern: pieces by file and range in R2 and the cache, made on demand, a request naming the stretch. Not started tonight: built in a hurry it risks every import the team makes.
